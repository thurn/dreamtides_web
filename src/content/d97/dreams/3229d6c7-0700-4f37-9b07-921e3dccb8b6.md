# Dream brief 3229d6c7-0700-4f37-9b07-921e3dccb8b6

The concept for this dream, written before its residents. The map module is
`src/content/d97/maps/3229d6c7-0700-4f37-9b07-921e3dccb8b6.ts`; every place
named in *italics* below is a landmark or region there. The dream has no
name. Its locals call it "the town", "up the hill" or "the valley", and
nothing else.

## Premise

A brick hill town on the last evening of harvest. The wheat is in, the long
tables are out, and the feast is about to begin. It has been about to begin
for as long as anyone can remember.

The sun hangs a hand's breadth above the ridge and does not move. Up the
barrel lane, at the head of *the high field*, stands *the ember arch*, a
horseshoe of slow fire on stone pillars. Everyone knows the sun goes down
through it at the end of the feast. Someone stands in its mouth, and the sun
will not pass them.

The town is not troubled by this. The bread is nearly risen, the fiddle is
nearly tuned, and the bell is nearly rung. Everyone is busy being almost
ready.

## Mood

- Amber light and long shadows. Cut straw, woodsmoke, warm bread, the noise
  of the falls under *the white bridge*.
- Content on the surface, with an undertow: an evening that has gone on far
  too long, and people too polite to say so.
- The farther from the square, the quieter it gets. *The herb ledge* and
  *the stubble terrace* feel like places the evening forgot.

## Tone note for the locals

This note applies the story bible (`docs/dreamtides-97/story-bible.md`) to
this dream's locals.

- **Plain country speech.** Unhurried and courteous. Short sentences, one or
  two to a line. No modern idiom, no slang, and no winking at the player.
- **Always about to.** They talk as if the feast starts any minute and always
  has. Nobody says the sun is stuck; they say "a long evening" or "it'll be
  dark soon enough".
- **Small wrongnesses, laughed off.** A loaf that will not finish rising, a
  count that always stops at the same number. They notice and move on.
- **Harvest sayings** stand in for opinions ("the last sheaf carries the
  luck", "never ring before the bread's out").
- **Warmth over whimsy.** The humor is dry, and the kindness is real.
- **Two motifs:** the almost (bread, a fiddle string, a toast, all nearly
  ready) and the count (a tally that always comes out the same). Widow
  Marrable alone expects the evening to end, and is at peace with it.
- They never name the town or the valley, and nobody here has heard of any
  other place by name.

## Landmarks

The town is *the well square*. A walker arrives through *the town door* in
the middle of it, and the inn, the hall, the well and the waypoint are all a
few steps away. Three broad ways lead out: north up *the barrel lane* to
*the high field*; south between the houses to *the south road*; and from the
south road down *the steps* to *the stubble terrace*.

## Residents

### The shopkeeper

**Wenna Ashdown**, the innkeeper, stands at *the counter* in *the inn*
and sells from it. She is broad, brisk and floury-sleeved, and keeps the
feast's first cup poured and waiting. She counts everything in barrels,
including time ("three barrels ago"). She sells cards as "provisions for the
road" and thinks anyone leaving before the feast is a little mad, though she
will gladly sell to them.

### Quest givers

- **Reeve Lambert Rook**, on *the hall steps*. Old, upright, fond of rules
  about bells. The feast cannot start until the feast bell rings, and the
  bell has lost its tongue. He is certain the boys rang it so hard it flew
  clean over the roofs.
- **Hessa Mott**, a young reaper at *the south corner*. Sharp, sunburnt and
  impatient with ceremony. By custom the last sheaf is plaited into a harvest
  maiden and carried to the feast. Hers blew away in a gust, "the only wind
  all evening".

### Townsfolk

Seven ambient characters, each on a townsfolk anchor: three in the square,
two in the inn, and two farther out.

1. **Pell the baker**, by the houses on the square's east side. Her loaves
   rise and rise and are never quite done. She is cheerful about it.
2. **Ambrose Finch**, a fiddler, north of *the well*. He has been tuning one
   string all evening and will play "the moment it's right".
3. **Dilly Sorrel**, a child on *the high field*. She counts sheaves and
   always loses count at the same number.
4. **Widow Marrable**, beside *the well*. She sets an extra place at every
   table "for whoever comes in from the dark". She is the only local who
   seems to expect night.
5. **Cobb**, the bridge-keeper at the head of *the white bridge*. He counts
   every crossing and hints at the steps, the barrel lane and the ledge
   nobody visits.
6. **Barnaby Thatch**, an old harvest hand by the west table in *the tap
   room*. His cup is full, and by his father's rule he will not drink before
   the bell.
7. **Nettie Furrow**, the inn's pot-girl, between the back tables in *the
   tap room*. Wenna has her set the feast cups out, and then set them out
   again.

### Challenger personas

Generic challengers for this dream, suited to its fields and hills:

1. **Threshers**: heavy-shouldered harvest hands with flails, on the south
   road. They play like they work: in rhythm.
2. **The Scarecrow**: straw-stuffed and grinning, down off its pole on the
   stubble terrace. It does not explain itself.
3. **The Tithe Clerk**: the reeve's man, ink-fingered with a ledger, met on
   the high field. He tallies your deck as he plays it.
4. **Hedge-witches**: herb-gatherers with sickles and bundled sprigs, on the
   herb ledge. They are amused by everything.
5. **Woodcutters**: axe-on-shoulder, sawdust-bearded, among the pines at the
   stubble terrace's west end. They are blunt and fair. Jory Hewitt speaks
   for them.

## Quest hooks

1. **The feast bell** (Reeve Lambert Rook). Find the bell's tongue on *the
   stubble terrace*, down *the steps* off the south road. Bringing it back
   lets the bell ring once, and the town cheers; the sun still does not
   move.
2. **The harvest maiden** (Hessa Mott). Find the plaited last sheaf, caught
   in the long grass at the far end of *the high field*, and carry it back
   to *the south corner*. Hessa sets it on the feast table.

The map also places two chests: one on *the west green*, one at the far end
of *the high field*. Neither stands behind the guardian.

## Through-line hooks

The dream reads the same in any act. Its light hooks, all on conditions the
dialogue schema has:

- **First meetings.** Wenna, the reeve and Cobb set a "met" flag on the
  first conversation and greet a returning player in one short line.
- **Quests.** Every townsperson reacts to the feast bell, the last sheaf or
  both; Cobb, Dilly and Nettie point the way while a quest is under way.
- **The guardian's fall.** Once whoever stood in the ember arch steps aside,
  Widow Marrable lights her lamp for the dark and Ambrose hurries his
  string: an ending in its time.
- **Depth.** In the deep dreaming (acts 4 and 5), Cobb sees the water run
  backward under the white bridge and Barnaby's hearth will not catch. Both
  laugh it off.
