# Dream brief 3229d6c7-0700-4f37-9b07-921e3dccb8b6

The concept for this dream, written before its residents. The map module is
`src/content/d97/maps/3229d6c7-0700-4f37-9b07-921e3dccb8b6.ts`; every place
named in *italics* below is a landmark or region there. The dream has no
name. Its locals call it "the town", "up the hill" or "the valley", and
nothing else.

## Premise

A brick hill town on the last evening of harvest. The wheat is in, the long
tables are out, and the feast is about to begin. It has been about to begin
for as long as anyone can remember.

The sun hangs a hand's breadth above the ridge and does not move. In the
middle of *the well square* stands *the ember arch*, a horseshoe of slow fire
on stone pillars. Everyone knows the sun goes down through it at the end of
the feast. Someone stands in its mouth, and the sun will not pass them.

The town is not troubled by this. The bread is nearly risen, the fiddle is
nearly tuned, and the bell is nearly rung. Everyone is busy being almost
ready.

## Mood

- Amber light and long shadows. Cut straw, woodsmoke, warm bread, the noise
  of the falls under *the white bridge*.
- Content on the surface, with an undertow: an evening that has gone on far
  too long, and people too polite to say so.
- The farther from the square, the quieter it gets. *The pine meadow* and
  *the low cave* feel like places the evening forgot.

## Tone note for the locals

This note stands in for the story bible until it exists.

- **Plain country speech.** Unhurried and courteous. Short sentences, one or
  two to a line. No modern idiom, no slang, and no winking at the player.
- **Always about to.** They talk as if the feast starts any minute and always
  has. Nobody says the sun is stuck; they say "a long evening" or "it'll be
  dark soon enough".
- **Small wrongnesses, laughed off.** A loaf that will not finish rising, a
  count that always stops at the same number. They notice and move on.
- **Harvest sayings** stand in for opinions ("the last sheaf carries the
  luck", "never ring before the bread's out").
- **Warmth over whimsy.** The humor is dry, and the kindness is real.
- They never name the town or the valley, and nobody here has heard of any
  other place by name.

## Residents

### The shopkeeper

**Wenna Ashdown**, the innkeeper, stands in front of the bar in *the inn*
and sells from it. She is broad, brisk and floury-sleeved, and keeps the
feast's first cup poured and waiting. She counts everything in barrels,
including time ("three barrels ago"). She sells cards as "provisions for the
road" and thinks anyone leaving before the feast is a little mad, though she
will gladly sell to them.

### Quest givers

- **Reeve Tobin Rook**, on *the hall steps*. Old, upright, fond of rules
  about bells. The feast cannot start until the feast bell rings, and the
  bell has lost its tongue. He is certain the boys rang it so hard it flew
  clean over the river.
- **Hessa Mott**, a young reaper at *the wheat corner*. Sharp, sunburnt and
  impatient with ceremony. By custom the last sheaf is plaited into a harvest
  maiden and carried to the feast. Hers blew away in a gust, "the only wind
  all evening".

### Townsfolk

Five ambient characters, each on a townsfolk anchor:

1. **Pell the baker**, by the houses on the square's east side. Her loaves
   rise and rise and are never quite done. She is cheerful about it.
2. **Ambrose Finch**, a fiddler, north of *the well*. He has been tuning one
   string all evening and will play "the moment it's right".
3. **Dilly Sorrel**, a child at the west end of *the great wheat field*. She
   counts sheaves and always loses count at the same number.
4. **Widow Marrable**, at *the creek well*. She sets an extra place at every
   table "for whoever comes in from the dark". She is the only local who
   seems to expect night.
5. **Cobb**, the bridge-keeper at the west end of *the white bridge*. He
   counts every crossing and hints at the fords, the plank bridge and the
   meadow nobody visits.

### Challenger personas

Generic challengers for this dream, suited to its fields and hills:

1. **Threshers**: heavy-shouldered harvest hands with flails, on the far
   bank. They play like they work: in rhythm.
2. **The Scarecrow**: straw-stuffed and grinning, down off its pole in the
   south fields. It does not explain itself.
3. **The Tithe Clerk**: the reeve's man, ink-fingered with a ledger, met on
   the high field. He tallies your deck as he plays it.
4. **Hedge-witches**: herb-gatherers with sickles and bundled sprigs, on the
   terraces. They are amused by everything.
5. **Woodcutters**: axe-on-shoulder, sawdust-bearded, in the stump field by
   the woodcutter's yard. They are blunt and fair.

## Quest hooks

Bead 1.15 authors these as data.

1. **The feast bell** (Reeve Tobin Rook). Find the bell's tongue in *the
   pine meadow*, reached through *the dry hollow* below the south fields.
   Bringing it back lets the bell ring once, and the town cheers; the sun
   still does not move.
2. **The harvest maiden** (Hessa Mott). Find the plaited last sheaf, caught
   in the grass at the north end of *the long meadow*, and carry it back to
   *the wheat corner*. Hessa sets it on the feast table.

The map also places two chests: one in *the woodcutter's yard*, one on *the
high field*. Neither stands behind the guardian.
