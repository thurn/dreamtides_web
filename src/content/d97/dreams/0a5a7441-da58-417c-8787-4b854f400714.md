# Dream brief 0a5a7441-da58-417c-8787-4b854f400714

The concept for this dream, written before its residents. The map module is
`src/content/d97/maps/0a5a7441-da58-417c-8787-4b854f400714.ts`; every place
named in *italics* below is a landmark or region there. The dream has no
name. Its locals call it "the oasis", "up the stair" or "the temple", and
nothing else.

## Premise

A palm oasis at the foot of sandstone cliffs, where a river comes down the
rocks in a long white fall. Above the palms, at the head of a stair cut into
the cliff, a temple portico stands open on the high sand. A water jar waits
on every doorstep in the oasis, filled each morning for whoever walks in out
of the heat.

Every morning the oasis sweeps the stair and the temple court smooth. Every
morning, by the time the sweepers reach the top, there is a single line of
footprints on the swept sand, walking up toward the temple. Nobody has ever
seen who makes them, and no footprints ever come back down. Someone stands
in the temple's mouth.

The oasis is not troubled by this. A guest is a guest. The jars are filled,
the stair is swept, and the footprints are greeted like a neighbor.

## Mood

- White heat and deep shade. Palm fronds, wet stone, the roar of *the falls*,
  dates drying on cloths, cool water in clay.
- Welcoming on the surface, with a hush underneath: a town that always seems
  to be expecting someone who has just gone ahead.
- The higher you climb, the stiller it gets. *The high terrace* and *the
  cliff nook* feel like places where the wind has stopped.

## Tone note for the locals

This note applies the story bible (`docs/dreamtides-97/story-bible.md`) to
this dream's locals.

- **Courteous, formal speech.** Measured sentences of even weight, one or
  two to a line. Few contractions among the old; more among the young. No
  modern idiom and no slang.
- **Hospitality first.** Every local offers water before anything else, and
  greets a doorway before stepping through it. Nobody turns a guest away.
- **The footprints, laughed off.** They say "our early guest" or "the one
  who goes ahead", never a ghost or a mystery, and change the subject to the
  sweeping.
- **Sayings of water and thresholds** stand in for opinions ("a full jar on
  the step, and the house is never empty", "greet the door, and the door
  greets you").
- **Warmth over whimsy.** The humor is dry and the kindness is real.
- **Two motifs:** thresholds (customs about doors, the gate and the temple
  mouth) and the extra place (the water jar on every step, filled for
  whoever comes). One old local alone expects the footprints to stop one
  day, and is glad of it.
- They never name the oasis, the river or the temple, and nobody here has
  heard of any other place by name.

## Landmarks

The town is *the oasis*. A walker arrives through *the town door* in *the
little gate*, a small portico in the cliff, and the awning, the domes and
the waypoint are all a few steps away among the palms. Two broad ways lead
out: up *the cut stair* between its lions to *the temple court*; and east
along the oasis floor to *the falls' foot*.

## Residents

Sketched here for the residents bead, which writes their looks, dialogue
and quests. Names follow the story bible's desert temple pattern.

### The shopkeeper

**Tahemet, the Water-Bearer**, stands before *the awning* and sells from
under it. She is tall, unhurried and ink-black of hair, and fills the jars
on every step in the oasis each morning. She sells cards as "water for the
road" and will not let a buyer leave thirsty.

### Quest givers

- **Iumeri**, a sweeper of the temple stair, on the path up from the palms
  to *the lower terrace*. Young, earnest and proud of a clean step. The jar that
  stands on the temple's threshold has gone missing, and the threshold must
  never be left dry.
- **Ubenet, the Scribe**, an old record-keeper beside *the south dome*.
  Precise and fond of lists. He keeps the tally of every guest the oasis
  has watered, and his tally board has slipped down to the pool.

### Townsfolk

Four ambient characters, three in the oasis:

1. **Neferibu**, a child on *the lower terrace*, who follows the footprints
   up the stair every morning and always loses them at the same step.
2. **Pabek**, a date-climber under *the tall palm*, who calls down news of
   the whole oasis from the top of the trunk.
3. **Old Ruamu**, who waters the travelers on the river shelf and hints at
   the stair, the nook and the bones nobody visits.
4. **Tiunet**, a weaver by *the west dome*'s door beside *the little gate*,
   who greets the gate every time she passes it, and expects the footprints to stop one day.

### Challenger personas

Generic challengers for this dream, suited to its sands and cliffs:

1. **Tomb-Wardens**: spear and painted shield, on *the high terrace*. They
   play as they stand guard: patiently.
2. **Sand-Diviners**: knucklebones and a bowl of sand, in *the cliff nook*.
   They read your hand before you play it.
3. **Reed-Cutters**: wet to the knee with sickles, at *the falls' foot*.
   They are cheerful and in a hurry.
4. **Scorpion-Charmers**: soft-spoken, with a covered basket. They are
   never quite where you left them.

## Quest hooks

1. **The threshold jar** (Iumeri). Find the temple's threshold jar on *the
   river shelf*, out past the temple court, and bring it back. Iumeri sets
   it on the threshold; the footprints still walk up in the morning.
2. **The tally board** (Ubenet). Find the scribe's tally board by the pool
   at *the falls' foot*, and carry it back to *the south dome*. Ubenet adds
   one more guest to the tally.

The map also places one chest, at *the high terrace*'s east end above the
stair. It does not stand behind the guardian.

## Through-line hooks

The dream reads the same in any act. Its light hooks, all on conditions the
dialogue schema has:

- **First meetings.** Tahemet, Iumeri and Old Ruamu set a "met" flag on the
  first conversation and greet a returning player in one short line.
- **Quests.** Every townsperson reacts to the threshold jar, the tally
  board or both; Neferibu and Old Ruamu point the way while a quest is under
  way.
- **The guardian's fall.** Once whoever stood in the temple's mouth steps
  aside, Tiunet says the footprints will rest now, and Neferibu finds them
  walking back down: an ending in its time.
- **Depth.** In the deep dreaming (acts 4 and 5), the jars are found empty
  each morning and the falls run quieter. Both are laughed off.
