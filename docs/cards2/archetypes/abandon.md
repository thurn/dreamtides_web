# Abandon — Deep Dive

Abandon is the archetype built around sending your own characters to the void on
purpose. The basic idea is the same one that has anchored sacrifice decks
forever: pair cards that abandon things for value with characters that are happy
to die, and let the loop pull ahead one body at a time. In Dreamtides this reads
through two mechanics. Abandon moves one of your own characters from play to the
void — it cannot be prevented, only targets your own side, and it fires that
character's ▸Dissolved trigger. And "when an ally is dissolved" payoffs reward
every loss, whether the body was abandoned to an outlet, traded in a challenge,
or eroded out from under you. The deck is the intersection of three things the
void wants to do: feed cheap fodder into outlets, convert each death into points
or cards, and recur the fodder so the next abandon is free. It comes in a fair
grind build that accrues value over many turns and an explosive "combo abandon"
build that storms off in one.

## The premier outlets

- Infernal Ascendant
- Saltless Mariner
- Soulrender

The single most important thing the deck needs is abandon outlets — a way to
turn a character into value on demand. You can have all the fodder and all the
payoffs in the world, but with nothing to abandon them to, the engine does not
turn over. A dedicated abandon list wants two or three reliable outlets at a
minimum, and the good ones go at a premium because they do more than just kill
your own bodies.

Infernal Ascendant ("Abandon another character: Gain 1●") is the workhorse.
With no energy cost on the ability and no limit on uses, it converts a stalled
board into energy and, more importantly, into a stream of ▸Dissolved and "when
an ally is dissolved" triggers. It is the outlet every loop in the combo build
is written around, and even in the fair deck it lets you cash a doomed body for a
point or a card by routing it through a payoff.

Saltless Mariner ("Veil 2●. Abandon a character: Gain 1⍟") is the premium outlet
because it carries its own payoff: every abandon is a point with no second card
required, and Veil 2● makes the engine itself hard to break up. Obliterator of
Worlds ("Abandon a character: Store 1⧗. X⧗: Dissolve an enemy with X✦") and its
cousin Kindlehorn ("When you abandon a character, store 1⧗. X⧗: Dissolve an enemy
with X✦") turn the same abandons into stored ⧗ and then into removal, so the
outlet doubles as a way to clear the opposing board. Soulrender ("Abandon a
character: Chosen player erodes X, where X is that character's cost") points the
deck's attrition at the opponent's deck instead, eroding them toward Fatigue each
time you feed it; abandon a 2● body and the opponent erodes 2.

The rest of the outlet suite trades the energy or point for a different resource.
Arc Disciple ("Abandon another character: Gain 2●") is pure acceleration, the
best ramp outlet in the pool. Thundercatcher ("2●, Abandon a 1✦ ally: Draw 2
cards") turns the deck's surplus of 1✦ bodies and figments straight into cards.
Ruptured Dynamo ("Abandon a character: Return a character from your void to your
hand") and Spirit Reaping ("Abandon a warrior: Gain 2●") round out a deck that
should rarely lack for an outlet once it is assembled.

## Fodder that wants to die

- Entropy Spike
- Spent Courier
- Conduit of Ashes

Once you have an outlet, you need things to feed it. The pool makes this easy:
most of the relevant characters are cheap and actively reward leaving play. The
biggest drafting mistake is loading up on expensive fodder — you want bodies
already in play before the outlet lands, so a board of one- and two-cost
characters that build value as they die is what you are after.

The best fodder pays you on the way out through a ▸Dissolved trigger. Entropy
Spike ("▸Dissolved: Choose one: Gain 1●. / Gain 1 maximum ●") is a 1● 1✦ body
that hands you energy — or permanent ramp — every time it is abandoned. Spent
Courier ("▸Materialized, Dissolved: Draw a card with ephemeral") and Ossuary
Overlord ("▸Materialized, Dissolved: Draw a card") replace themselves with a card
on both entry and exit, so they are card-neutral fodder at worst. Searcher in the
Mists ("▸Materialized, Dissolved: Erode 4") and Ashwalker ("▸Materialized: Erode
3. ▸Dissolved: Return a character from your void to your hand") fill the void as
they cycle through it, which both fuels Soulrender's erosion clock and stocks the
recursion targets the deck wants.

Recurrent bodies are what make the feeding sustainable. Cinderfang ("▸Dissolved:
You may pay 1● to return this character to hand") bounces back to hand whenever
it is abandoned, ready to be replayed and fed again. Duskwall Delver ("When an
ally is dissolved, you may pay 1● to materialize this character from your void")
climbs back out of the void off any death, including its own. Conduit of Ashes
("Abandon this character: Gain 1●") is a 0● 0✦ body that is both fodder and a
free outlet for itself — central to the combo build below. Twilight Reclaimer
("▸Dissolved: A survivor in your void gains reclaim until end of turn") turns each
abandon into permission to replay a survivor out of the void, and Kindred Sparks
("With an allied survivor, you may play this card from your hand or void for 1●")
is the recurrent survivor those reclaims want — a 1● body that does not care which
zone it lives in.

## Death-trigger payoffs

- Silent Avenger
- Fathomless Maw
- Skull Weaver

The fair deck wins by converting all that dying into points or cards. The drains
are the clock. Silent Avenger ("When a character is dissolved, gain 1⍟") is the
core payoff: every abandon, every challenge trade, every loop iteration is a
point, and it counts opposing dissolves too. Fathomless Maw ("When you abandon a
character, gain 1⍟") prices each abandon at a point on its own, and Skull Weaver
("When an ally leaves play, gain 1⍟") catches even the bodies that bounce to hand
or banish rather than dissolve. Cinderheart ("When you abandon a character, this
character gains +1✦. ▸Dissolved: Gain ⍟ equal to this character's ✦") banks the
abandons as spark and then cashes the whole pile as ⍟ when it finally dies
itself — feed it into your own outlet for the payout.

The card-draw payoffs keep the engine fed instead of pointed at the opponent.
Sunset Chronicler ("When an ally is dissolved, draw a card") and Soulreaver
("When a non-figment ally is dissolved, draw a card") turn every loss into a
card, while Deathwalker ("When an allied survivor is dissolved, draw a card") and
Scuttled Fortune ("Draw a card for each ally that dissolved this turn") reward a
turn that abandons several survivors at once. Volcanic Channeler ("When an ally
is dissolved, gain 1●") rewards the attrition with energy, refunding the next
play in the loop. Seer of the Fallen ("When a non-figment ally is dissolved,
create 'Contemplation' in your hand") builds card selection out of the same
deaths.

The figment-makers turn attrition into an army. Wreckborn ("When a non-figment
ally is dissolved, materialize a 1✦ ember figment. When an ember figment is
dissolved, this character gains +1✦"), Dustborn Veteran ("When a non-figment
allied survivor is dissolved, materialize a 1✦ survivor figment"), and Standard
Bearer ("When you play a character, materialize a 1✦ warrior figment") replace
every fed body with a fresh figment, which itself becomes the next thing you
abandon. Because same-type figments stack into one position and absorb spark from
the top down, the army these build is a real board presence and not just fuel.

## The fair grind build

- Infernal Ascendant
- Silent Avenger
- Dustborn Veteran

The fair build is not trying to win in one turn. It assembles an outlet, a death
payoff, and a board of cheap fodder, then grinds: each turn it abandons a body or
two for energy and a point, the payoffs fire, and figment-makers leave the board
no smaller than it started. Played this way the deck plays at the intersection of
attrition and tempo — Obliterator of Worlds and Kindlehorn keep the opposing
board clear with their stored-⧗ removal while Silent Avenger ticks up points, and
the figment army eventually goes wide enough to score spark directly in unpaired
lanes.

The void and survivor themes support this half heavily. Harvester of Despair
("Abandon another character: This character gains +1✦") is a recurring growth
body that turns spare fodder into a threat. Carrion Shepherd ("▸Dawn: Give an
allied wraith +1✦. With no allied wraiths, materialize a 0✦ wraith figment with
vengeful instead") seeds vengeful wraiths the deck is glad to feed an outlet —
each dies fighting and triggers your payoffs. Most games you simply build a pile
that grows every time something dies until it consumes the opponent; the tighter
the outlet-plus-drain pairing, the faster you get there.

## The combo abandon build

The combo build wants the same pieces but uses them to go as big as possible in a
single turn and storm off, rather than accruing value across many. The goal is to
loop a free body through a free outlet, firing a death payoff each iteration,
until a drain or an erosion outlet has run away with the game.

### Energy and free outlets

- Infernal Ascendant
- Arc Disciple
- Melodist of the Finale

A combo turn needs energy to keep going. Infernal Ascendant and Arc Disciple are
the engines, converting each abandoned body into 1● or 2●; Spirit Reaping does
the same for warriors. Melodist of the Finale ("Veil 2●. When you play a
character, gain 1●") refunds the replay half of any loop that recasts a body, and
Entropy Spike's "▸Dissolved: Gain 1●" pays you for abandoning it. The deck wants
its loops to be at least energy-neutral so they can run as long as there is a
payoff to point at.

### Loops that recur the fodder

- Conduit of Ashes
- Fading Farewell
- Nexus Wayfinder

The cleanest loop is Fading Farewell ("Until end of turn, when an ally is
dissolved, return it to your hand") with a free body and a free outlet. Abandon
Conduit of Ashes through its own "Abandon this character: Gain 1●" ability: you
gain 1●, Fading Farewell returns the abandoned Conduit to your hand, you replay
it for 0●, and abandon it again. Each iteration is energy-positive and fires every "when an ally
is dissolved" trigger on the board, so a Silent Avenger or Fathomless Maw on the
table turns it into a point per loop and Soulrender turns it into runaway erosion.

A second loop skips the return spell entirely. Nexus Wayfinder ("Characters with
cost 2● or less cost you 0●") makes any small body free to replay, and Ruptured
Dynamo ("Abandon a character: Return a character from your void to your hand") is
a no-energy outlet that fishes the abandoned body back out of the void. Abandon a
2●-or-less character to Ruptured Dynamo, return it, replay it for 0● off Nexus
Wayfinder, and abandon it again — the loop costs nothing and rains down ▸Dissolved
and "when dissolved" triggers, closed by the same drains.

The figment producers feed loops that do not even need a recurring body. Call of
the Lost ("For each card you've played this turn, materialize a 1✦ ember figment
with awakened. Reclaim 2●") and Packcaller of Shadows ("For each card you've
played this turn, materialize a 1✦ ethereal figment") spit out a wide board of
fodder after a long turn, and Radiant Trio ("Materialize X 1✦ ethereal figments")
makes them directly. Shadowbinder ("☪: Materialize two 1✦ ember figments with
awakened. Abandon them at end of turn") hands you two free bodies a turn whose
end-of-turn abandon you would rather take early through an outlet. Each of those
figments is a free abandon into your drains, and Wreckborn and Dustborn Veteran
turn the abandons back into more figments to keep feeding.

### Mass-return engines

- Wake the Fallen
- Shadow March
- Heroic Rescue

The biggest combo turns route through mass-return effects. Wake the Fallen
("Materialize all characters in your void which dissolved this turn"), Shadow
March ("Materialize all characters with cost 2● or less in your void which
dissolved this turn"), and Heroic Rescue ("Materialize up to three chosen characters
in your void which dissolved this turn") bring back everything you have abandoned
this turn at once, re-firing every ▸Materialized and ▸Dissolved trigger so you can
abandon the whole board again. Pair them with an outlet and an engine like The
Forsaker ("Abandon a character with cost 2● or less: Gain 1⍟") or Ferryman of the
Falls ("When a character with cost 2● or less is put into your void, draw a card")
and a single return refills an exhausted loop into another full lap.

### Winning the turn

Closing the combo turn is the easy part. Silent Avenger or Fathomless Maw turns
the loop into a flood of ⍟ outright. Soulrender erodes the opponent into Fatigue.
Kindlehorn and Obliterator of Worlds bank stored ⧗ from each abandon and machine
down the opposing board, then the figment army you built scores its spark in
unpaired lanes. Lanternwood Scout ("1●, Abandon another character: Store 1⧗.
Abandon this character: Gain X⍟ where X is the stored ⧗") banks a long chain of
abandons into stored ⧗ and then cashes them all as a single burst of points.
Cinderheart does the same with spark, paying out its whole accumulated total when
you abandon it last.

## Warrior and void overlap

- Soulrender
- Spirit Reaping
- Burning Revenant

The warrior theme supplies a parallel set of outlets and recursion that slots
straight into the deck. Spirit Reaping and Arc Disciple are warrior outlets;
Dreaming Obelisk ("☪, Abandon a warrior: Materialize a warrior from your void")
and Pit Descender ("☪, Abandon a warrior: Materialize a warrior with cost 2● or
less from your void") are "welder" outlets that loop a warrior through the void
for value. Burning Revenant ("When an allied warrior is dissolved, return a
warrior with lesser cost from your void to hand"), Starbound Striker ("▸Dissolved:
Return another warrior from your void to your hand"), and Tranquil Duelist
("Vengeful. ▸Dissolved: Return another warrior from your void to your hand") make
abandoning warriors self-replacing. Fathomscourge ("Once per turn, when an allied
warrior is dissolved, materialize a 1✦ warrior figment") and Voidsire-style
figment makers keep the warrior board wide, and Smoldering Ancient ("When an
allied warrior is dissolved, this character gains +1✦. ☪, Abandon a warrior: Gain
1⍟") is both a payoff and an outlet in one warrior body.

The void theme contributes the recursion that keeps fodder coming. Shadowcaller
("Veil 3●. When an ally is dissolved, materialize a 1✦ survivor figment. When you
discard or erode this card, it gains reclaim 0●") makes a figment on every death
and replays itself for free once it has been milled. Twilight Reclaimer and
Kindred Sparks turn the void into a renewable fodder supply, Path to Redemption
("Until end of turn, cards in your void gain reclaim") lets a stocked void come
back all at once, and Borrowed Minutes ("Materialize each character with cost X●
or less in your void. Banish them at end of turn") drops a temporary board you are
glad to abandon before it banishes anyway.

## Misc. role players

- Door to Possibility
- Rite of Summoning
- Pulse of Sacrifice

The deck appreciates a little selection to find its outlet. Door to Possibility
("Choose a value of X. Discover an event with cost X") and Rite of Summoning
("Discover a card with cost X●") dig up the missing piece — an outlet, a drain,
or a mass-return spell — when the loop is one card short. Aftermath Bloom ("To
play this event, abandon a warrior: Discover a warrior with higher cost, then
materialize it") doubles as a warrior tutor that is itself an abandon, trading a
small body up the curve. Pulse of Sacrifice ("Discard your hand. Gain 3●") is a
ritual that refuels a stalled storm turn at the cost of cards you may have been
discarding for value anyway.

Removal that overlaps the engine is welcome. Molten Duel ("Dissolve a character
with 3✦ or less") and Forsaken Skyline ("Dissolve an enemy with 2✦ or less. When
you discard this card, it gains reclaim 1● until end of turn. Reclaim 4●") clear
small bodies, the latter coming back from the void. Fury of the Clan ("Play this
card only with 3 allied warriors. Dissolve an enemy") is free removal once the
figment army is wide. Colossal Convergence ("❖❖ – ☪, Abandon a character: Give an
ally +X✦ where X is the abandoned character's ✦") and Forsworn Champion ("❖❖ –
Abandon a warrior: This character gains +1✦") let you abandon at Interrupt speed
to spring a challenge in your favor, converting a dying body into the spark that
wins a lane.

## Overlap with other archetypes

Abandon shares its outlets and recursion with several decks. The Characters with
Cost 2● or Less deck runs the same Nexus Wayfinder, Ruptured Dynamo, The
Forsaker, and Ferryman of the Falls to loop cheap bodies for value. Survivors
leans on the same drains and ▸Dissolved fodder, since most of the cheap abandon
targets happen to be survivors. The Warrior Combo deck uses Spirit Reaping,
Dreaming Obelisk, Pit Descender, and Aftermath Bloom to loop warriors. And the
mass-return combos — Wake the Fallen, Shadow March, Heroic Rescue — are shared with
the figment and recursion decks. Whatever the table leaves open, an abandon core
of an outlet, a drain, and a board of cheap bodies will find a home.
