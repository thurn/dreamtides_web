# Discard / "Madness" — Deep Dive

Discard / "Madness" is a void-fueled value strategy that turns throwing cards
away into an advantage. On the surface the "madness" mechanic is simple: certain
cards read "When you discard this card, it gains reclaim," so discarding them is
not a loss — it parks a card in the void that you can play from there instead.
The end result is virtual card advantage, because discarding is usually attached
to a cost paid for some other benefit. If you get value on both sides of that
cost — the benefit of the discard outlet and the payoff of the discarded card —
you are deep in value town.

The mechanics are straightforward; the hard part is the constant big-picture
decisions that come with funneling your hand into the void as early as the first
or second turn. Discarding doubles as void setup, so the archetype is really two
overlapping plans at once: a grindy card-advantage engine and a void-matters
deck that pivots into recurring or void-scaling threats.

## The "madness" cards

The signature cards gain reclaim the instant they hit the void from a discard,
so an outlet that would otherwise cost you a card is paying you instead. Part
the Veil is the marquee example: a 4● event that erodes 4 and lets you put two
of those cards into your hand, and "When you discard this card, it gains reclaim
2● until end of turn" — so discarding it to an outlet turns it into a 2● dig you
play straight from the void that same turn. Oblivion Guide is the cheap creature
version, a 1● explorer that gains reclaim 0● until end of turn when discarded, so
it lands on the board for free after being thrown away. Ridge Vortex Explorer
goes one step further — "When you discard this card, materialize it" — so the
discard *is* the deployment, no reclaim payment required.

The reclaim-when-discarded text appears across both events and characters, which
is what lets the discard outlets feed real board presence and real spells.
Forsaken Skyline (dissolve an enemy with 2✦ or less; gains reclaim 1● when
discarded) turns a thrown-away card into cheap removal from the void. Sorrowful
Prince is a 2● warrior with "X●: This character gains +X✦ until end of turn,"
the pumpable madness body that comes down off a discard outlet and immediately
threatens to score. Pinnacle Ascendant rewards both lines: it gives allied
characters awakened, and "When you discard or erode this card, it gains reclaim
0●," so a card you discarded or milled comes back as a free awakening anthem.

## Discard outlets

The first thing the deck wants is an outlet — a repeatable way to convert cards
in hand into cards in the void on demand. Evacuation Enforcer is the premier
engine, the closest thing to a free repeatable outlet: a 1● Awakened trooper
with "☾: Draw 2 cards, then discard 3 cards." It enters awakened so it works the
turn it lands, and each activation loots two-for-three, churning three cards into
the void every turn. It is the central signal to be in the archetype, and it
turns any card in your hand into raw void fuel.

Pulse of Sacrifice is the most explosive outlet. For 0● it discards your entire
hand and gains 3● — so it dumps every madness card you are holding into the void
*and* pays you a burst of energy to immediately play them back from there. An
opening of Pulse of Sacrifice into a hand of Oblivion Guide, Sorrowful Prince, and
Part the Veil discards everything for three energy, then redeploys the lot off
the reclaim it just handed out: a free board and a dig, on turn one. It is the
ritual that powers the fast starts.

Curio Dealer is the toolbox outlet, a 2● tinkerer with Veil 2● and "1●, Discard
a character with cost X●: Discover a character with cost X●." It discards a
creature to find the exact creature you need, so it fills the void and tutors at
the same time — discard a madness body, dig up the next one, and so on, building
the void while you find your pieces. Wheel effects are the mass outlets:
Starfall Communion has each player discard their hand and draw 5, and Wheel of
the Heavens shuffles both hands and voids into the decks and draws 5. Both reload
you mid-game and dump a fresh hand of madness cards into the void on the way.

For the more incidental outlets, Spiral Offering pays its discard up front — "To
play this event, discard 2 cards" to draw 3 — so it is card advantage that
happens to fill the void with two cards. Sunken Radiance (draw 2, discard 2,
Reclaim 2●) loots and recurs itself. Shadowpaw is a 1● body whose "☾, Discard a
card: Erode 2" doubles a discard with two milled cards, deepening the void fast.
Liminal Dreamer turns a stored count into a refill — it stores 1⧗ each Dawn and
"Discard your hand: Draw one card per stored ⧗," emptying your hand into the void
to draw a fresh one. It is difficult to keep a discard plan running without
something permanent and repeatable, so even with a few one-shot looters in your
deck you want to find an Evacuation Enforcer or a Curio Dealer.

## Discard payoffs

These are the cards that get value from the discard itself, rather than from the
card that was discarded. Minstrel of Falling Light is the strongest: a 2● body
with "☾, Discard an event: Draw an event" and "This character has +1✦ for each
event in your void." It loots events for free, grows as the void fills with the
events it discards, and becomes a sizable threat in a deck doing exactly what it
wants. Key Sifter pays the same way from the other angle — "Once per turn, when
you discard one or more cards, draw that many cards" — so the first discard each
turn, from any outlet, replaces itself. With Key Sifter out, Evacuation
Enforcer's three discards draw you three cards, and a Wheel effect's whole hand
comes right back.

Flagbearer of Decay is the open-ended payoff: a 2● warrior with "When you
discard a card, you may play that card from your void this turn." It triggers off
*any* discard, with no limit on what you play — discard a Part the Veil and a
Sorrowful Prince to Evacuation Enforcer and you can play both from the void this
turn. Crack a Pulse of Sacrifice with Flagbearer in play and the entire
discarded hand becomes playable. Shoreline Penitent is the smaller, self-fueling
version — "3●, ☾, Discard a card: Draw a card" plus "When you discard a card, it
gains reclaim until end of turn" — so it is both an outlet and a payoff, handing
reclaim to whatever it discards. Gleamharvester pays in spark and tempo: "When
you discard a card, this character gains +2✦ until end of turn," and on
▸Challenge it may discard a card to draw a card, turning a busy discard turn
into a real attacker.

The repeatable free-discard creatures make all of this card-positive. Silent
Gatherer returns itself from the void to hand each Dawn, so it is an unlimited
discard target — feed it to an outlet every turn for free. Resilient Wanderer
does the same with a 1● tax. Wandering Archivist closes the gap to points
directly: "2●, ☾: Draw a card, then discard a card" loots, and "When you discard
a card, gain 1⍟" means every discard, from any source, also drains the opponent.
Rubble Diviner is the ▸Challenge looter — "You may pay 2● and discard a card to
draw two cards" — pulling cards while it attacks. Stack a free-discard creature
like Silent Gatherer with a payoff like Key Sifter or Wandering Archivist and
each turn's discard becomes pure card advantage or pure points.

## Pivoting into void threats

Because discarding doubles as void setup, the deck does not just grind value — it
funnels specific cards into the void deliberately and then cashes them in as
threats. The first kind recurs from the void on its own. Harrowing Officiant is a
5● ancient with "When you play your second card in a turn, materialize this
character from your void," so once it is in the void — discarded or eroded there
— it keeps coming back every turn you play two cards, which a discard deck does
trivially. The Rising God is the top-end recursive threat: an 8●
ancient with "Reclaim – 2●, Abandon two characters," so you discard it into the
void early and bring it back later for two bodies and 2●.

The second kind scales off a full void. Paradox Enforcer is a 1●, 7✦
visitor that you play *from your void* by banishing 7 cards from it — a discard
deck fills the void to that depth quickly, then deploys a huge
threat for a single energy. Forgotten Titan is a 4●, 5✦ ancient that costs 3●
less if you have discarded a card this turn, so a routine discard turns it into a
1● body. Vessel of Echoes has +5✦ with six or more cards in your void and carries
Reclaim 1●, and Architect of Memory has +4✦ and gains awakened with seven or more
cards in the void — both flip from chaff into live threats once the discarding
has done its work. Steel Abomination grows whenever a cost-2●-or-less character is
put into your void and returns two of them on ▸Dissolved, rewarding the constant
flow of small cards into the void.

The third kind wants to be in the void as an anthem. Soulkindler is a 4● visitor
that does nothing notable in play but reads "If this card is in your void, allied
characters have +1✦" — so you actively discard it to turn the void into a
team-wide spark boost. Pinnacle Ascendant and Pyrestone Avatar (an Offering body
that gives allied characters awakened) reward the same instinct: cards you want
in the void or on the board cheaply, deployed off a discard. The deck's threats
are pointed at the void, so every discard outlet is also a deployment engine.

## Winning the game

Once the engine is humming, there are a few ways to actually close. The most
aggressive is to weaponize the discards themselves. Wandering Archivist gains 1⍟
on every discard, so a Wheel effect or a few Evacuation Enforcer activations
quietly drain the opponent. Fleeting Reunion is the dedicated finisher — an
X-cost event that gains X⍟ with "Reclaim – Discard X cards" — so once your value
engines have filled your hand you discard a pile of cards from the void to gain a
matching pile of points. Silent Avenger turns the deck's natural attrition into a
clock: "When a character is dissolved, gain 1⍟" punishes every challenge while
your recurring threats trade up.

The second plan is inevitability. Recurring bodies like Harrowing Officiant, The
Rising God, and the void-scaling threats above are very hard to grind through —
no matter how the opponent answers them, they keep coming back from the void. Add
Silent Avenger or Wandering Archivist as the drain and the deck simply
out-resources the table over a long game.

## The wheel and erode package

Wheel effects are the archetype's mass refill, and they overlap with the Storm
direction. Starfall Communion and Wheel of the Heavens reload a depleted hand
while filling the void, so they are at their best in a deck that is happy to have
discarded everything. Across the Void (shuffle hand and void into deck, draw 5;
Reclaim – 2●, Abandon a character) is the recurring wheel that resets your void
when it gets too thin, then comes back to do it again. These pair with the copy
engine — Cascade of Reflections (until end of turn, when you play an event, copy
it) doubles a wheel into a draw-10, the same way the Storm deck uses it.

Erode is the alternative path into the void: rather than spending a card from
hand, you mill cards straight off the top, which feeds void-count payoffs without
the card disadvantage. Searcher in the Mists erodes 4 on ▸Materialized and again
on Dissolved, and Depthwalker erodes 4 and lets you return a cost-2●-or-less
character from among those cards to hand. Ashwalker erodes 3 and returns a
character on Dissolved. Several madness cards key on erode as well as discard —
Pinnacle Ascendant and Shadowcaller both gain reclaim 0● "When you discard or
erode this card" — so an eroded copy is just as live as a discarded one. Whisper
of the Past loops itself by eroding 3 to put itself back on top of the deck, and
Pathwalker erodes 3 from the void each Dawn, deepening the void turn after turn.

## Survivor overlap

The discard and void plans run straight through the survivor package, which is
the deck's most natural creature base. Shadowcaller is the keystone bridge piece:
a 3● survivor with Veil 3●, "When an ally is dissolved, materialize a 1✦ survivor
figment," and "When you discard or erode this card, it gains reclaim 0●." You
discard it to the void as setup, replay it for free, and it turns the deck's
attrition into a growing board of figments. Sorrow Watcher and Tidewreck
Navigator make survivor figments and draw cards, and Tidewreck Navigator's "☾,
Discard a card: Materialize a 1✦ survivor figment" is itself a discard outlet
that builds the board. Veil of the Wastes returns itself from the void to play
whenever you materialize a survivor — a recurring body the discard deck fuels for
free. Kindred Sparks ties it together: with another survivor in play it plays from
hand or void for 1●, so it is happy being discarded and bought back cheaply.
Wasteland Arbitrator is the incidental outlet of the package — a 1●-reclaim
survivor whose ▸Materialized makes each player discard a card, so it disrupts the
opponent while feeding your own void.

## Recursion and reuse

The pool rewards getting extra mileage out of each card you commit to the void.
Self-returning bodies — Silent Gatherer, Resilient Wanderer, Veil of the Wastes —
are inexhaustible discard fodder. Reclaim events give you repeat interaction and
draw: Sanctum Awakened (draw 2, Reclaim 2●), Sunken Radiance (loot, Reclaim 2●),
and Shatter the Frail (dissolve an enemy with 1✦ or less, Reclaim 1●) all come
back from the void for a second pass. Scrap Reclaimer is the recursion engine —
"2●, ☾, Discard a card: Return up to 2 characters with cost 2● or less from your
void to your hand" — a single outlet that loots a card and refills your hand with
two void creatures, so the discard deck never runs out of bodies to redeploy.
Pathwalker abandons itself to return an event from the void, and Steel
Abomination returns two small characters on Dissolved, keeping the engine stocked
even as it trades in challenges.

## Role players and incidental fits

A handful of cards slot in around the edges. Forsaken Pact is the void-and-tutor
play — "To play this card, abandon a character with cost 2● or less" then
"Discover a character with cost 2● or less and materialize it" — putting a body in
the void and pulling a fresh one onto the board. Emberwatch Veteran turns a
discard into removal of a sort, gaining vengeful for the turn so it trades up in a
challenge. Duneveil Vanguard converts a discarded small character directly into
removal — "Discard a character with cost 2● or less: Dissolve an enemy with cost
2● or less" — pointing the deck's discards at the opponent's board. Urban Cipher
is a self-contained wheel-on-a-body, discarding your hand and drawing 3 on
▸Materialized, and Verdant Wayfarer draws 2 and recurs from the void by banishing
7 cards from it, a payoff the deep void enables. Soulbinder and Dreaming Obelisk
bridge into the Abandon and Warrior loops respectively, and Harborwarden ("☾,
Discard a card: Materialize a 1✦ warrior figment") is a discard outlet that
builds a warrior board for the aggressive builds.

## How to draft and play it

Take the outlet first — Evacuation Enforcer is the signal, with Curio Dealer and
the Wheel effects as the mass options and Pulse of Sacrifice as the explosive
enabler. Then pick up the payoffs that make the discards card-positive: Key
Sifter, Minstrel of Falling Light, and Flagbearer of Decay are the cheap engine,
Wandering Archivist and Shoreline Penitent the value glue. Lean on the
reclaim-when-discarded cards (Part the Veil, Oblivion Guide, Ridge Vortex
Explorer, Sorrowful Prince, Forsaken Skyline, Pinnacle Ascendant) so that every
discard parks a live card in the void. Decide on a finish: the aggressive build
weaponizes the discards with Wandering Archivist, Fleeting Reunion, and Silent
Avenger and closes with Sorrowful Prince and the survivor figments, while the
inevitability build leans on recurring and void-scaling threats — Harrowing
Officiant, Paradox Enforcer, The Rising God — that the opponent cannot grind
away. Across both builds the discard does double work: it is your card-advantage
engine and your void setup at once, with victory emerging from the steady stream
of cards you throw away and then get back better than they left.
