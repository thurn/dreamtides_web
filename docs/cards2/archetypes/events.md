# Events — Deep Dive

Events is a small but easily overlooked archetype. It is an events-matter shell:
a high density of cheap interactive events — removal, prevents, hand disruption,
and card draw — paired with a handful of payoff characters that trigger whenever
you play an event. The events are the deck; the payoffs are secondary. A core of
eight or more 1● and 2● events makes a solid backbone on its own, and the
payoffs reward you for playing them. This same core slots into a variety of
directions, so Events splashes naturally into Storm, Discard, and control.

## The interaction is the deck

The reason Events works at all is the sheer volume of cheap, interactive cards
in the pool. With the curve so low, a single timely prevent on a key enabler or
payoff can set the opponent back many turns, and the cheap removal keeps the
front rank clear while your own clock advances.

First, the removal. Because most characters are small, a cheap dissolve is a
reliable answer. Molten Duel dissolves a character with 3✦ or less for 1●,
Shatter the Frail and Unleashed Destruction clear early bodies for 1●, Scorched
Reckoning dissolves an enemy with 3✦ or less, and Unleash Ruin dissolves
an enemy with cost 3● or less. Grounded is the flexible catch-all: an X-cost
event that banishes an enemy whose cost is exactly X●, so it scales to answer
whatever the opponent committed.

Next, the prevents. Counters in this pool are always Interrupts, so they can be
held up and fired in response while you spend the rest of the turn doing other
things. Toll of Passage prevents a played card with cost 2● or less for 1● — with
the curve so low this hits a large fraction of what the opponent will cast.
Defiant Parry and Echoing Denial both carry Offering, so they can be played for
0● by banishing a card from your hand, letting you protect your plan without
tapping out: Defiant Parry prevents a played card unless the opponent pays 1●,
and Echoing Denial prevents a played card outright. Infernal Rest and Momentum of
the Fallen round out the soft-counter suite at 1●.

Then there is hand disruption, a natural pairing with prevents — seeing the
opponent's hand tells you when to hold up interaction and when to advance your
own board, and you get to strip their answers. Lurking Dread takes a chosen card
with cost 2● or less from the opponent's hand for 1●; Vertiginous Leap and Peak
Plunder take any chosen card, and Peak Plunder carries Reclaim 1● so it returns
from the void for a second pass. Break the Veil is the recursive option — the
opponent discards a card, and it has Reclaim – Discard a card, so it grinds the
opponent's hand turn after turn.

Finally, card draw to keep the events flowing. Epiphany Unfolded draws 3 cards
for 1●, Fragments of Vision and Gleam Below refill cheaply, and Guiding Light and
Ancient Descent bundle Foresee with a cantrip so you smooth your draws while
feeding the engine. Door to Possibility and Inverted Reflection let you Discover
the exact interactive event you need. The biggest concern for an Events deck is
running out of gas after trading off resources one-for-one, so these cheap
refills are what keep you from getting out-valued.

## The payoffs

The payoff characters all read "When you play an event, …", so every cheap event
you were already casting does double duty. Gateweaver is the workhorse: a 2●
body that materializes a 1✦ ethereal figment each time you play an event, so a
turn of interaction quietly builds a wide board of figments. Somber Flockmaster
plays from the other direction — on ▸Dawn, an event in your void with cost less
than or equal to its ✦ gains reclaim 0● until end of turn, recurring a spent
removal spell or prevent for free.

Echo Architect is the marquee payoff: events cost you 1● more, but when you play
an event, copy it. Doubling a Molten Duel, a Lurking Dread, or a draw event more
than pays for the tax, and it overlaps directly with the Storm package below. The
Waking Titan and Duskshore Virtuoso both store 1⧗ when you play an event and turn
that count into a payoff — Duskshore Virtuoso spends 3⧗ to gain +5✦ and become a
real threat, while The Waking Titan spends 5⧗ to return all other characters to
hand, a board-resetting blowout after a busy turn of events. Both want the same
thing the rest of the deck wants: cast a lot of cheap events.

Without the events these payoffs do nothing, but the events are perfectly fine on
their own. So take the cheap interaction first and pick up the payoffs later,
until you have at least eight solid 1● and 2● events.

## Recursion and reuse

The pool rewards getting extra mileage out of each interactive event. Somber
Flockmaster, covered above, hands free reclaim to a void event each Dawn. Keeper
of the Lightpath does the same on demand — ☾ gives an event with cost 3● or less
in your void reclaim, so you can buy back a prevent or a removal spell at the
moment you need it. Whisper of the Past is the cheapest enabler of all: for 1● it
gives an event in your void reclaim until end of turn, and it loops itself back
onto the deck by eroding. Several of the interactive events carry their own
recurrence — Peak Plunder (Reclaim 1●) and Break the Veil (Reclaim – Discard a
card) keep coming back, so a small set of disruptive events represents a much
larger stream of interaction over a long game.

## The "spell-copying" core (Storm splash)

The strongest events-matter cards are the copy effects, which is exactly where
Events bleeds into Storm. Echo Architect copies every event passively. Cascade
of Reflections does it for a turn — until end of turn, when you play an event,
copy it — so it is the dedicated go-off enabler. The Ringleader and Cosmonaut of
Tides set up single big copies: Cosmonaut of Tides copies your next cost-2●-or-
less event twice on ▸Materialized, and The Ringleader creates a 0● ephemeral copy
of your next event in your hand. Stacked together these copy effects accumulate
favorably, multiplying a cheap removal spell or draw event into a swing.

The energy to chain them comes from the same rituals the Storm deck uses: Glimpse
of Infinity (0●, gain 3●), Canopy of Stars and Arc Gate Opening (gain 2● / 4●),
Flash of Power (gain 5●), Genesis Burst (double your current ●), and Nebula's
Wake (gain 5● once you have three events in your void — trivially true in this
deck). The chain is then cashed out: Broadcast Array gains 2⍟ for each card you
have played this turn and cannot be prevented, Intermezzo Balladeer gains 1⍟ for
each other card you've played this turn every time you play a card, and Echoes of
the Journey refills the chain by drawing a card with ephemeral for each card you
have played this turn, all costing 0● that turn. An Events shell with a copy
engine is one Cascade of Reflections away from a real Storm turn.

## Discard splash

Because so many Events cards are loots and the deck is happy to throw spent cards
into the void for later reclaim, it slides into the Discard direction. Sunken
Radiance (draw 2, discard 2, Reclaim 2●) is a natural inclusion that both digs
and fills the void. Wheel-style refills like Starfall Communion (each player
discards their hand, then draws 5) and Wheel of the Heavens reload your event
suite mid-game, and the discarded events feed Somber Flockmaster, Keeper of the
Lightpath, and Whisper of the Past for reclaim value. Part the Veil and Break
the Veil are events that reward discard outright. Shadowcaller is the bridge
piece — a survivor that gains reclaim 0● when discarded or eroded — letting the
discard subtheme convert thrown-away cards into board presence while the event
engine hums.

## Control splash

The interactive core leans naturally toward a controlling game. An Events deck
can play slow for the first several turns, leaning on its glut of cheap removal
and prevents to one-for-one the opponent while it sets up. The card-draw events
keep the hand full so the control plan does not run dry, and the payoffs become
the win condition: Duskshore Virtuoso grows into a threat off stored ⧗, Gateweaver
goes wide with figments, and The Waking Titan resets the board when things get
out of hand. Grounded and Echoing Denial give the control build clean,
scalable answers to whatever the opponent's plan top-ends at.

## Awaken overlap

The events-matter trigger also powers the Cindermarch / Shadow Soloist awaken
engines, so a heavy-events build overlaps with those loops. Shadow Soloist
awakens each allied character whenever you play an event, turning a chain of
cheap events into a fully awakened board mid-turn. Gearwright loots — draw a card,
then discard a card — every time you play an event, and Moonlit Dancer gives
allied characters +1✦ until end of turn on each event, so a flurry of cheap
interaction also pushes spark onto the front rank. Lanternhearted (☾: Awaken an
ally) provides repeatable awakens to keep ☾ producers online while the events
flow. These pieces are the awaken backbone shared with the Storm and Abandon
loops rather than a standalone plan, but they show how naturally the cheap-event
core feeds the rest of the pool.

## How to draft and play it

Take the 1● and 2● interaction first — the removal (Molten Duel, Flashpoint
Detonation, Grounded), the prevents (Toll of Passage, Defiant Parry, Echoing
Denial), the hand disruption (Lurking Dread, Peak Plunder, Break the Veil), and
the cantrips (Epiphany Unfolded, Guiding Light). These are fine on their own and
slot into Storm, Discard, Outsider tempo, and control alike. Wheel the payoffs
later: Gateweaver and Somber Flockmaster as the cheap engine, Echo Architect and
the copy package as the ceiling, and Duskshore Virtuoso or The Waking Titan as
the threats that close. The plan is to grind the opponent's board and hand to
nothing with a stream of cheap events, while the payoffs quietly convert that
same interaction into figments, copies, spark, and points — victory emerging as a
byproduct of out-interacting the opponent rather than from a dedicated finisher.
