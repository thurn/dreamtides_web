# Survivors

Survivors is the premier value archetype of the void. It was not built as a
tribal beatdown deck — it emerged because so many of the best void enablers
happen to share the Survivor subtype, and a handful of dedicated payoffs were
added on top. The result is a deck that plays at the intersection of everything
the void wants to do: discarding cards, abandoning characters, and recurring
threats out of the void faster than the opponent can answer them. When this
writeup refers to "Survivors" it means the overall gameplan first and the
subtype only second. Most of the cards the deck wants to play are survivors
anyway, so the tribal payoffs come essentially for free.

## The premier enablers

- Kindred Sparks
- Sorrow Watcher
- Dustborn Veteran

Kindred Sparks is THE survivor card. It is hard to overstate how central it is.
Printed as a 4● 2✦ survivor, it carries "With an allied survivor, you may play
this card from your hand or void for 1●." Once any survivor is on the board it
becomes a 1● body that does not care which zone it lives in — replay it from
hand, let it dissolve, replay it again from the void, over and over. With so
many ways to abandon characters, erode yourself, and convert recursion into
cards, this one card does everything. Taking it late is a sign Survivors is open
and a cue to move in. It loops cleanly with an abandon outlet such as Infernal
Ascendant ("Abandon another character: Gain 1●") — abandon Kindred Sparks for
1●, replay it from the void for 1●, and the energy washes out while every "when
an ally is dissolved" and ▸Materialized trigger fires again. With a draw payoff
like Deathwalker or a drain like Silent Avenger attached, that neutral loop
turns into raw card advantage or points.

Sorrow Watcher is the enabler that keeps the engine fed. A 1● 1✦ survivor, its
▸Materialized trigger materializes a 1✦ survivor figment, and its ☪ ability
draws a card for each 3 allied survivors. It builds the board, refills the hand,
and — because every survivor figment it makes counts toward later payoffs — it
quietly scales the whole deck. There is real tension in when to commit it:
played early it starts banking figments and triggers, but holding it lets you
land it into a board where the draw ability comes online immediately.

Dustborn Veteran is the payoff that turns the deck's natural attrition into a
growing army: "When a non-figment allied survivor is dissolved, materialize a
1✦ survivor figment." Every survivor that dies in a challenge, gets abandoned to
an outlet, or trades away leaves a figment behind. There are many ways to go
deep with this, most of them involving the deck's abandon outlets — Infernal
Ascendant, Dreadweaver, Soulrender, Kindlehorn — each abandon of a non-figment
survivor netting a fresh body. Stacked survivor figments share a position and
absorb spark from the top down, so the army it builds is also a genuine board
presence.

The dedicated card-draw payoffs round this out. Deathwalker ("When an allied
survivor is dissolved, draw a card") and Soulreaver ("When a non-figment ally
is dissolved, draw a card") convert every loss into a card; Thundercatcher
("2●, Abandon a 1✦ ally: Draw 2 cards") turns the deck's surplus of 1✦ bodies
and figments directly into pairs of cards.

That is the whole tribal toolkit — there are no survivor lords and no dedicated
beatdown plan. The strength of the deck is that the payoffs come for free
alongside cards you wanted anyway.

## The game plan

- Kindred Sparks
- Shadowcaller
- The Rising God

Survivors leans into whatever the table leaves open, and the pool is flexible
enough to support two distinct builds within one archetype. The abandon build
treats the board as fuel: abandon outlets and recurrent bodies feed each other
until the pile gets too big to answer. The discard build plays more like a void
reanimator, eroding and discarding to fill the void and then casting threats out
of it for a discount. Many cards bridge both halves — Wasteland Arbitrator
("▸Materialized: Each player discards a card." plus Reclaim 1●) is a disruptive
body that also feeds the void, and Shadowcaller makes a survivor figment every
time an ally is dissolved while gaining reclaim 0● the moment it is discarded or
eroded.

### The abandon build

Here you prioritize good abandon outlets and recurrent threats. Infernal
Ascendant is the workhorse outlet, turning each abandoned character into 1●;
Dreadweaver ("2●, ☪, Abandon two other characters: This character gains +2✦.
Draw 2 cards.") is a top-end engine that empties a stalled board into a pump and
two cards. Volcanic Channeler ("When an ally is dissolved, gain 1●") rewards the
attrition with energy on every loss, fueling the next replay of Kindred Sparks.

Recurrent bodies are what make abandoning sustainable. Duskwall Delver ("When an
ally is dissolved, you may pay 1● to materialize this character from your void")
returns itself for 1● whenever anything dies, and Cinderfang ("▸Dissolved: You
may pay 1● to return this character to hand") bounces back to hand to be replayed.
The Rising God is the lategame closer: an 8● 7✦ Unstoppable Ancient with
"Reclaim – 2●, Abandon two characters", it comes back out of the void for 2● and
two bodies you were happy to feed an outlet anyway, then scores its full spark
directly as ⍟ when it goes unanswered.

The deck wins this way by converting the loop into points or cards. Silent
Avenger ("When a character is dissolved, gain 1⍟") and Fathomless Maw ("When you
abandon a character, gain 1⍟") turn the attrition into a clock — each abandon of
Kindred Sparks for the Infernal Ascendant loop is a point. Most games you simply
build a pile that grows every time something dies until it consumes the
opponent; the tighter loops (Kindred Sparks + Infernal Ascendant + a drain) get
there faster.

### The discard build

The discard build plays like a void-recursion deck: erode and discard to load
the void with threats, then cast them out for a discount or for free. Searcher
in the Mists ("▸Materialized, Dissolved: Erode 4") and Ashwalker ("▸Materialized:
Erode 4" plus "▸Dissolved: Return a character from your void to your hand") fill
the void and then mine it. Evacuation Enforcer ("Awakened. ☪: Draw 2 cards,
then discard 3 cards") and Urban Cipher ("▸Materialized: Discard your hand, then
draw 3 cards") are the heavy discard outlets that set up the void in a hurry.

The cards you want to be discarding are ones that get cheaper, better, or simply
free from the void. Vessel of Echoes is a 1● 1✦ survivor that has +5✦ with six
or more cards in your void and carries Reclaim 1●, so a full void turns it into a
6✦ body you replay for 1●. Shadowcaller and Pinnacle Ascendant ("Allied
characters have awakened") both gain reclaim 0● when discarded or eroded, so
pitching them is upside rather than a cost. Soulkindler ("If this card is in
your void, allied characters have +1✦") actively wants to be in the void — it is
a board-wide pump that does its job from the discard pile. Twilight Reclaimer
("▸Dissolved: A survivor in your void gains reclaim until end of turn") turns
each death into permission to replay something out of the void.

Prioritize discard and erosion outlets in this build, because a card like
Soulkindler or Shadowcaller is dead stranded in hand. Several of the disruption
events double as self-discard: Break the Veil ("The opponent discards a card.
Reclaim – Discard a card") and Peak Plunder ("Discard a chosen card from the
opponent's hand. Reclaim 1●") can be aimed at the opponent while their reclaim
modes let you re-buy them from the void. Path to Redemption ("Until end of turn,
cards in your void gain reclaim") is the payoff for a stocked void — a single
turn where everything you have milled becomes castable at once.

## Closing the void out

- Paradox Enforcer
- The Rising God

A void full of survivors wants a finisher that turns all that material into a
win. Paradox Enforcer ("To play this character, banish 7 cards from your void.
You may play this character from your void. Unstoppable") is a 1● 7✦ Unstoppable
body that the discard build's eroding naturally pays for, and you can cast it
straight out of the void. Verdant Wayfarer ("▸Materialized: Draw 2 cards.
Reclaim – 3●, Banish 7 cards from your void") similarly converts a deep void into
cards. The Rising God closes the abandon build the same way, recurring for 2●
and two abandons to land a 7✦ Unstoppable threat that scores its spark as ⍟.

## Misc. role players

- Vertiginous Leap
- Peak Plunder
- Unleash Ruin

The deck benefits hugely from incidental disruption. Vertiginous Leap ("Discard
a chosen card from the opponent's hand") is a clean 1● strike at the opponent's
plan, and Peak Plunder does the same with a reclaim mode so it keeps coming back.
A single well-aimed discard can be game-defining, and these small hindrances —
strip a card, abandon a body, erode the deck — add up to a grinding, attritional
advantage.

Removal is cheap and recurrable. Unleash Ruin ("Dissolve an enemy with
cost 3● or less") clears most of the curve for 2●. Shatter the Frail ("Dissolve
an enemy with 1✦ or less. Reclaim 1●") trades for small bodies and comes back
from the void to do it again. Whisper of the Past ("An event in your void gains
reclaim until end of turn") plus its self-recurring clause lets you keep these
events flowing turn after turn.

Some bodies advance disruption while also feeding the engine. Wasteland
Arbitrator hits both players' hands on entry, fills your void, and reclaims for
1●. Voidcaller ("▸Materialized: Banish an enemy warrior") is a tempo body against
Warrior decks. Sky Voyager ("This character has all character types. ▸Dawn: Gain
1●") counts as a survivor for every payoff while also ramping you, and
Momentum's Edge ("▸Materialized: Choose a character type. This character is that
character type. Characters of the chosen type have +1✦") names Survivor to become
both a body and a board-wide pump.

## Acceleration and utility

- Ruin Scavenger
- Lanternhearted
- Pathwalker

Survivors can stall without some form of acceleration, and a few cards provide it
alongside utility. Ruin Scavenger ("☪, Banish a card from the opponent's void:
This character gains +1✦") doubles as void hate against opposing recursion and a
growing body. Lanternhearted ("Awakened. ☪: Awaken an ally. Reclaim 1●") fixes
the deck's tempo problem — characters enter exhausted, and Lanternhearted lets a
freshly replayed Kindred Sparks or a returned recurrent body act the turn it
arrives, then reclaims itself for 1●.

Pathwalker ("Abandon this character: Return an event from your void to your hand."
plus "▸Dawn: If this card is in your void, erode 3") is a utility piece that
fills the void from the discard pile every Dawn and buys back a key event when
abandoned, so it can go into the deck with little intention of being cast from
hand. Whisper of the Past plays a similar role on the event side, recurring
itself onto the deck for the cost of eroding three cards — exactly the resource
the discard build is happy to spend. Casting a recurred card out of the void
costs you no card from hand, so a "free" event replayed from the void is pure
advantage on top of the value you already got when it went there.
