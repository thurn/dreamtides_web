# Blink — A Dreamtides Archetype Deep Dive

Blink is probably a little different than what most are expecting — it is the
archetype this pool gets the most questions about. Rather than a deck built
around one massive game-winning loop, Blink is a value sub-archetype. There is
not really a "Blink deck"; there are decks that run Blink synergies, and the
difference between them is mostly in how they win. The core idea is small and
repeatable: a rematerialize effect re-fires an in-play character's ▸Materialized
trigger, so a body that drew a card or banished an enemy when it entered does it
again, for free. Pile up enough of those re-fires and you pull steadily ahead in
cards and board, then convert that lead into ⍟ with whatever finisher the rest
of your deck happens to be.

Because the curve of this pool tops out low, most of the rematerialize targets
do something close to drawing a card. That makes Blink synergies a great way to
grind out card advantage, but it also means you will usually need a win
condition planned beyond "rematerialize my Frost Visionary a bunch of times."
The engine keeps your hand full; something else has to actually score.

## The rematerialize line

The mechanic the archetype is named for is a single rules term shared across its
enablers. Rematerialize triggers an in-play character's materialization again,
firing its ▸Materialized ability and any "When you materialize" triggers. Read
against the rules, that is the whole engine: a character that draws a card, or
banishes an enemy, or returns a card from your void on entry will repeat that
effect every time it is rematerialized, and it does so without leaving play, so
the body stays on the board the entire time. Nothing about it goes through the
stack, nothing costs energy, and the character keeps any spark it has gained.

There is a closely related backup line — returning a character to hand and
replaying it. A bounce sets up a fresh ▸Materialized trigger the next time the
card is played, which is slower than a rematerialize (you pay the energy again,
and the card sits in hand a turn) but works on bodies the dedicated enablers
cannot reach and folds neatly into the same shell. Both lines do the same job:
re-buy enter-play value turn after turn.

## Enablers

- Passage Through Oblivion
- Duskmount
- Starlight Guide

These three are the premier rematerialize enablers. Passage Through Oblivion is
the cheapest and cleanest: a 1● event that reads "Rematerialize an ally" and
carries "Reclaim 1●," so after you play it from hand you can replay it from your
void for 1● and fire a second ▸Materialized. One card, two re-fires of your best
entry effect — point both at Winterbough Monk and you have returned two cards
from your void, or at Dusk Duelist to dissolve two enemies.

Duskmount is the engine you build around. It enters Awakened, so it can act the
turn it lands, and its ability is "☾: Rematerialize an ally. This character
gains +1✦." Every turn it exhausts to re-fire a chosen ally's ▸Materialized and
grows by +1✦ while it does so, turning into a real threat on the side. Aim it at
Glimmerwood Scout for a recurring banish, or at Frost Visionary for a card a
turn, and you have a value engine that asks for no further cards and gets bigger
as it runs.

Starlight Guide puts the effect on a 2● body with "▸Challenge: Rematerialize an
ally." It only fires when Starlight Guide is itself a challenger — committed to
the front rank as of the end of your Day phase — so it asks you to send it into a
lane, but in exchange the rematerialize comes free with combat. Send it forward,
re-fire a value entry, and let it trade or score.

For a backup rematerialize-adjacent line, several bodies return an ally to hand
on entry, which sets up the bounce-and-replay version of the loop. Still Dreamer
("▸Materialized: Return an ally to hand") and Forgotten Factory Titan, which is
Awakened and reads "▸Materialized: Return an ally to hand," both pick up a spent
value body so you can recast it for another ▸Materialized — and because Forgotten
Factory Titan and Still Dreamer can each return *themselves* when nothing better
is around, they keep cycling their own bounce. Key to the Moment is the mass
version: "Return all but one allied character to hand. Draw a card for each
character returned." Bounce your whole team of ▸Materialized bodies, draw a fat
stack of cards, and replay them all for a second round of entry triggers. This is
slower than the dedicated enablers and works best once your energy is high, but
it is a real refuel in a stalled game.

## Value engines

- Winterbough Monk
- Frost Visionary
- Dreamvale Monarch

The premier rematerialize targets are the card-advantage bodies, and the pool is
full of them. Frost Visionary and Dreamvale Monarch both read "▸Materialized:
Draw a card" on a cheap 2● body — the bread and butter of the deck, since every
re-fire is another card. Winterbough Monk is the value standout: "▸Materialized:
Return a card from your void to your hand." Rematerialize it and you are
recurring your best spell or character out of the void every turn, which in a
grindy game is a soft lock on resources. Loop Winterbough Monk with Passage
Through Oblivion and you can buy back the Passage itself, then keep going.

- Verdant Wayfarer
- Call to the Unknown
- Featherlight Summoner

Bigger entry effects reward the dedicated enablers more. Verdant Wayfarer is a
5● body with "▸Materialized: Draw 2 cards" — a heavy refuel that Duskmount or a
reclaimed Passage Through Oblivion turns into four or six cards across a couple
of re-fires. Call to the Unknown reads "▸Materialized: Discover a character with
2✦ or less," letting each re-fire dig three cards deep for exactly the body you
want next, which is how you keep the engine fed. Featherlight Summoner makes a
4✦ monstrosity figment on entry ("▸Materialized: Materialize a 4✦ monstrosity
figment"); rematerialize it and you are stamping out big bodies, a way for a
value engine to actually build a board that can score rather than only drawing
cards.

- Rift Pilgrim
- Barrage Specialist
- Pilgrim of Old Stones

A few more entry effects round out the value suite. Rift Pilgrim ("▸Materialized:
Look at the top 4 cards of your deck and banish one. ▸Dissolved: Put the banished
card into your hand") is card selection now that pays off later. Barrage
Specialist discovers an event with cost 2● or less on entry, fetching a prevent
or a cheap removal event — exactly the interaction this deck wants more of.
Pilgrim of Old Stones is the disruptive one: "▸Materialized: Discard a chosen
card from the opponent's hand. They draw a card," so each re-fire strips their
best card before they can use it. As a 2✦ body it is also a fine ally to bounce
or rematerialize for repeat hand attack.

## Removal on a body

- Dusk Duelist
- Glimmerwood Scout
- Voidcaller

Some of the strongest rematerialize targets are removal stapled to a creature,
because re-firing them answers a threat every turn for free. Dusk Duelist
dissolves an enemy with 2✦ or less on entry; Glimmerwood Scout banishes an enemy
with cost 4● or less (mind that it hands the opponent a card when it leaves play,
so keep it on the board and rematerialize rather than bouncing it); Voidcaller
banishes an enemy warrior, a sharp answer when Warrior Aggro is across the table.
Point Duskmount at any of these and you have recurring removal that costs no
cards and grows your Duskmount each turn.

- Ambush Operative
- Cosmic Puppeteer
- Bladefall

The premium removal extends the suite. Ambush Operative is a 5● body with
Offering and "▸Materialized: Banish an enemy" — you can drop it for 0● by
banishing a card from hand to banish a threat at a key moment, and even though
Offering banishes it at end of turn, you got the entry effect (and a re-fire is
welcome if you can squeeze one in first). Cosmic Puppeteer reads "▸Materialized:
Gain control of an enemy with cost X● or less," stealing a threat outright and
swelling your own board — a backbreaking effect to re-fire. Challenge of the
Blade is the 1● clean answer, "Banish an enemy," for the slots where you just
need to remove something and get on with your plan.

## Going wider and looping

- Cindermarch
- Conduit of Resonance
- Nexus Wayfinder

A handful of cards turn the steady value into something faster. Nexus Wayfinder
reads "Characters with cost 2● or less cost you 0●," and almost every cheap value
body in this archetype — Frost Visionary, Dreamvale Monarch, Still Dreamer,
Pilgrim of Old Stones — falls under that line. With it down you can dump a string
of ▸Materialized bodies for free, firing entry trigger after entry trigger in a
single turn. Pair it with the bounce enablers and you can replay the same free
bodies repeatedly, a self-fueling draw engine while Nexus Wayfinder stays in
play.

Cindermarch ("When you materialize a character, awaken each other allied
character") and Conduit of Resonance ("When you play a character, trigger the
'▸Dawn' ability of each other allied character") both reward the many-materialize
turns Blink naturally produces. Cindermarch un-exhausts your whole board every
time a character enters, which keeps Duskmount and Starlight Guide ready and lets
☾-cost bodies fire more than once; with a free body from Nexus Wayfinder you can
materialize repeatedly and keep the board awakened. Conduit of Resonance turns
each character you play into a board-wide ▸Dawn pulse — with energy producers
like Cloudmantle Ray and Sky Voyager out, every fresh body refills your energy,
which can chain into still more bodies on the same turn.

- Cloudmantle Ray
- Sky Voyager

The energy bodies smooth the whole thing out. Cloudmantle Ray ("▸Dawn: Gain 1●")
and Sky Voyager ("▸Dawn: Gain 1●") are the dorks that accelerate your value plays
out a turn early, exactly the role the low curve wants filled. Cloudmantle Ray
also carries "Reclaim 3●," so a dead one in the void comes back. Sky Voyager has
all character types, which makes it count for any tribal payoff your shell
splashes and a flexible ally for bounce and rematerialize lines. Both turn into
real engines under Conduit of Resonance, where each character you play re-fires
their ▸Dawn for another point of energy.

## Folding in Outsiders

- Northlight Maestro
- Vanishing Inquisitor
- Thronebound Arbiter

The Outsider bounce bodies slot beautifully into a Blink shell. Each reads
"▸Materialized: Return another ally to hand. Move this character to that ally's
position," which is a built-in bounce enabler — return a spent value body to
hand, set up its next ▸Materialized, and slide the Outsider into a front-rank
lane in one play. They also draw when they score: Northlight Maestro and
Vanishing Inquisitor draw on scoring ⍟ (Inquisitor filtering with a discard),
and Thronebound Arbiter draws whenever any allied outsider scores. Running a
value-heavy board of ▸Materialized bodies is exactly what these want, since every
one of them is another thing to bounce and re-buy. Roots of Rebirth ("Materialize
two random characters with cost 2● or less from your deck") fits the same shell,
slamming two cheap bodies into play and firing both their entry triggers at once.

## Winning the game

This is not an archetype to go all-in on the synergy unless you pair it with
something else, because the rematerialize engine does not close a game on its
own — it keeps you ahead on cards and lets you trade favorably until your lead is
overwhelming. The win usually comes as a side effect of grinding the opponent
out: re-fire removal (Dusk Duelist, Glimmerwood Scout, Voidcaller) and hand
attack (Pilgrim of Old Stones) until they have nothing, draw a wall of cards off
the value bodies, and then convert the board into ⍟ in open lanes.

The bodies that do the actual scoring vary with the build. Featherlight Summoner
stamps out 4✦ monstrosity figments that score in unpaired lanes, and Duskmount,
which gains +1✦ every time it rematerializes, grows into a finisher on its own.
The Outsider package gives a sharper close: slide a scoring body into an empty
front-rank lane to convert its spark to ⍟ while Northlight Maestro, Vanishing
Inquisitor, and Thronebound Arbiter refill your hand so the grind never runs dry.
Because trading one-for-one always favors the player drawing extra cards each
turn, the plan is to keep the opponent in check with re-bought interaction until
you can deploy more than they can answer.

It pays to take disruptive cards highly when you build this way. Drawing many
cards per turn is great when you spend those cards keeping the opponent honest —
the cheap removal events and the discover-an-event body (Barrage Specialist) earn
their slots by making sure the value engine has time to take over. If your
interaction is being cut, the energy dorks are the next best thing: if you cannot
slow the opponent down, you may as well speed up your own engine.

## Overlap with other archetypes

Blink's value bodies and rematerialize line overlap heavily with Outsiders, which
runs the same ▸Materialized engines and the bounce ability; the difference is
that Outsiders is a tempo-control shell built on cheap interaction, where Blink
leans on the re-fire loop for raw card advantage. Duskmount and the ▸Materialized
removal bodies (Glimmerwood Scout, Voidcaller, Cosmic Puppeteer) are at home in
both. Nexus Wayfinder, the cost-2●-or-less free engine, and the energy dorks
splash into any low-curve creature deck. Cindermarch and Conduit of Resonance
reach across into the awaken-and-replay loops, where their board-wide effects pay
off the same many-materialize turns. The through-line is that Blink is rarely the
whole deck — it is the value layer you fold into a creature shell that already has
a way to win.
