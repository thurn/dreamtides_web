# Warrior Combo

Warrior Combo is the controlling, combo-oriented side of the warrior theme, and
one of the deepest engines in the pool. It takes the same wide-warrior shell as
Warrior Aggro and bends it toward recursion: a warrior in your void is worth far
more than a single body, because the right pair of pieces can return it to hand
or to play over and over, draining ⍟, eroding the opponent's deck, or simply
burying them in card and energy advantage. Where Warrior Aggro wants to deploy
and swing, Warrior Combo wants to loop. The two archetypes share most of their
cards — the difference is the win condition.

The core is always the same trio: a **recursion engine**, an **abandon outlet**,
and a body cheap enough to feed back into the loop faster than it leaves. Add a
drain or erosion payoff and the loop becomes a kill.

## Recursion

- Burning Revenant

For the most part, Warrior Combo centers on returning warriors from your void
back into the game, and the best card for that is Burning Revenant: "When an
allied warrior is dissolved, return a warrior with lesser cost from your void to
hand." Abandon (or trade away) a warrior of cost N and you pull a warrior of cost
less than N out of the void and into your hand. This is enormously powerful,
because any single warrior in hand is effectively worth a descending chain of
warriors — abandon the cost-4, get back the cost-3; abandon that, get back the
cost-2; and so on down to a 0● body like Conduit of Ashes or Dread Arbiter. (The
returned card need not be exactly one cost lower — getting a 2● warrior back off
a 4● one is fine; the strict descent is just the maximal line.) Each step is a
▸Dissolved trigger and a fresh card in hand, so even one abandon outlet turns
Burning Revenant into a value avalanche.

- Starbound Striker
- Infernal Cavalier
- Tranquil Duelist

These three are Burning Revenant's best friends. Each returns a warrior on its
own ▸Dissolved trigger — and because Abandon fires ▸Dissolved, you control
exactly when they fire. Starbound Striker is the cheapest: "▸Dissolved: Return
another warrior from your void to your hand." Infernal Cavalier returns "another
warrior with cost 4● or less," covering all but the very top of the curve.
Tranquil Duelist adds Vengeful on top of "▸Dissolved: Return another warrior from
your void to your hand," so it trades up in combat and still refills you on the
way out. With one of these plus Burning Revenant in play, a single abandon yields
two warriors back to hand at once — the one the Striker/Cavalier/Duelist returns,
and the lesser-cost one Burning Revenant returns — which is how a loop becomes
net-positive on cards.

- Prism Caller

Prism Caller is the other premier recursion engine: "When you play a warrior, you
may materialize a character with cost 2● or less from your void." This one isn't
limited to abandoning bodies — every warrior you play, from hand or from the
void, drags a cheap character straight back into play (not to hand), firing its
▸Materialized trigger for free. In a deck full of cheap warriors with
▸Materialized payoffs (Spent Courier, Ossuary Overlord, Sigil Analyst) Prism
Caller turns each warrior you cast into two materializations of value.

## Abandon Outlets

- Spirit Reaping

Spirit Reaping is the gold-standard abandon outlet for Warrior Combo, and the
card you most want to find and tutor toward, because it enables loops nothing
else can: "Abandon a warrior: Gain 2●." There is no ☾ cost and no per-turn
limit, so you can feed it any number of warriors in a single turn, each abandon
firing ▸Dissolved (and therefore Burning Revenant), each one paying you 2● back.
This is what converts a board of cheap warriors into a near-bottomless pool of
energy and triggers.

- Arc Disciple
- Infernal Ascendant

Arc Disciple ("Abandon another character: Gain 2●") and Infernal Ascendant
("Abandon another character: Gain 1●") are the redundant copies — both are
repeatable, free-to-activate abandon outlets that pay energy. Arc Disciple
matches Spirit Reaping's 2● rate but can abandon any character, not only
warriors; Infernal Ascendant pays only 1● but is the same uncapped engine. When
the bodies you abandon refund more energy than the loop costs, these outlets are
what push the deck from "great value" into "do this until the opponent is dead."

- Forsworn Champion

Forsworn Champion is a slower, body-building outlet: "❖❖ – Abandon a warrior:
This character gains +1✦." It is the least energy-efficient of the outlets — it
pays in spark, not energy — so it shines in a more aggressive shell where it is a
fine card on its own and you happen to have a recursion engine for value. With
Burning Revenant beside it, every +1✦ also refills your hand. It will not power a
truly infinite loop by itself, but as an Interrupt-speed outlet it can grow into
a lethal challenger across a single combo turn.

## Welders

- Dreaming Obelisk
- Pit Descender

The welders return warriors directly to play rather than to hand. Dreaming
Obelisk reads "☾, Abandon a warrior: Materialize a warrior from your void," and
Pit Descender reads "☾, Abandon a warrior: Materialize a warrior with cost 2● or
less from your void" (with a ▸Materialized: Erode 3 attached to seed your void).
They are happy to endlessly recycle warriors with ▸Materialized and ▸Dissolved
triggers — abandon Ossuary Overlord ("▸Materialized, ▸Dissolved: Draw a card") to
a welder and you draw on the way out and draw again on the way back in. Because
each activation costs ☾, a lone welder cycles once per turn; the way to multiply
it is to awaken it again (Crescendo Channeler's board, Wolfbond Chieftain) or run
both welders together so two warriors return each turn. Pit Descender's Erode 3
also stocks the void with fresh targets, making it the better standalone setup
piece.

## Tutors

- Aftermath Bloom
- Mirror Protocol

Aftermath Bloom is the archetype's warrior tutor: "To play this event, abandon a
warrior. Discover a warrior with higher cost, then materialize it." You feed it a
small warrior — which itself fires ▸Dissolved, triggering Burning Revenant or a
Striker — and dig up a larger piece, putting it straight into play. It is both a
combo enabler and a fail-safe, trading a 0● body for whichever engine you are
missing. Mirror Protocol does the same upward climb on a stick: "☾, Abandon a
warrior: Discover a warrior with cost 1● higher and materialize it." Repeated
across turns it assembles a chain of successively larger warriors directly onto
the board, doubling as the tutor that finds Spirit Reaping or Burning Revenant in
the first place.

## Board and Energy Generation

- Forge-Twin
- Crescendo Channeler
- Voidsire
- Spellweaver

These four keep the warrior count high, which feeds every loop above (more
warriors to abandon, more triggers off Prism Caller and Voidsire) and every
warrior anthem the deck borrows from Warrior Aggro. Forge-Twin materializes a
figment copy of Blade of Unity ("▸Materialized: Materialize a figment copy of
'Blade of Unity'"), a warrior that has +1✦ for each allied warrior —
a free body that scales with the board. Voidsire reads "When you play a warrior,
materialize a 1✦ warrior figment," so every warrior you cast widens the board by
another figment, and those figments stack into a single growing position.

Crescendo Channeler is the energy engine: "▸Dawn: Materialize a 1✦ warrior
figment" plus "2●, ☾: Gain 1● for each allied warrior." On a wide warrior board
that activated ability nets far more energy than it costs, and the energy refunds
fuel another lap of abandon outlets and recursion. Spellweaver ("1●, Abandon a
non-figment warrior: Materialize a 1✦ warrior figment") converts your real
warriors into figment fodder while firing a ▸Dissolved each time, a steady
abandon outlet that also keeps the board wide for Skyflame Commander and Momentum's
Edge.

## Win Conditions

- Silent Avenger
- Soulrender
- Nineborn Specter
- Twilight Suppressor
- Junkfield Renegade
- Intermezzo Balladeer
- Burst of Obliteration
- Terminus

Closing the game once a loop is running is the easy part — you just need the loop
to push ⍟ across the table or empty a deck. Silent Avenger ("When a character is
dissolved, gain 1⍟") is the cleanest: every abandon in the loop is a dissolve,
so a near-infinite abandon chain is near-infinite ⍟. Soulrender ("Abandon a
character: Chosen player erodes X, where X is that character's cost") turns the
same chain into deck erosion, grinding the opponent into Fatigue. Nineborn
Specter ("▸Dissolved: Gain 2⍟") rewards the bodies you sacrifice, and Twilight
Suppressor banks the engine ("When you play a character, store 1⧗" / "1⧗, Abandon
a character: Gain 1⍟"), converting both halves of the loop into points. Junkfield
Renegade is a small, self-contained closer ("1●, Abandon this character: Gain
2⍟" or draw a card) that doubles as recursion fodder.

Intermezzo Balladeer ("When you play a card, gain 1⍟ for each other card you've
played this turn") rewards the replay-heavy turns the loop generates — each
warrior recast from hand stacks the count higher. Burst of Obliteration is a
flexible finisher (Gain X⍟ or dissolve an enemy with X✦) once your energy pool is
overflowing. And Terminus ("If you have no cards in your deck, you win the
game") is the deterministic out for any build that erodes itself empty while
filling its void — a natural pair with Soulrender pointed at yourself and Pit
Descender's Erode 3.

Ideally your closers are warriors so they fold back into the recursion engines:
Soulrender, Nineborn Specter, Twilight Suppressor, and Junkfield Renegade all
carry the warrior type, so Burning Revenant and the welders can return them too.

## Re-deriving the Loops

The loops are where literal thinking fails, so trace them from the real text.

**Burning Revenant + abandon outlet (the value chain).** With Burning Revenant in
play and Spirit Reaping as the outlet, abandon any warrior: Spirit Reaping pays
2●, the abandon fires ▸Dissolved, and Burning Revenant returns a lesser-cost
warrior to your hand. Replay that warrior (cheaply — Tethered Hollow makes the
first warrior each turn cost 3● less, Sundown Ronin and Dreadmount Sovereign cut
warrior costs), abandon it again, and step down the cost chain. Each lap is a
card and a ▸Dissolved trigger; add Silent Avenger and each lap is also 1⍟. This
is not strictly infinite on its own, but with a Striker/Cavalier/Duelist also in
play you net two returns per abandon, so the chain refills faster than it drains.

**Welder loops (return to play).** Dreaming Obelisk abandons a warrior and
materializes one from the void, firing the returned body's ▸Materialized trigger.
Pair it with Ossuary Overlord or Spent Courier and each lap draws a card; pair it
with Burning Revenant and the abandon half also returns a lesser warrior to hand.
The ☾ cost caps a single welder at one lap per turn, so the engine wants a
re-awaken (Crescendo Channeler keeping the board wide and energy flowing) or both
welders at once.

**Prism Caller storm.** "When you play a warrior, materialize a character with
cost 2● or less from your void." Each warrior cast pulls a cheap body back into
play for free. With Voidsire and Forge-Twin widening the board and Crescendo
Channeler refunding energy, a single combo turn can replay a long sequence of
cheap warriors — and Intermezzo Balladeer turns that sequence into a mounting ⍟
total, while Twilight Suppressor banks a ⧗ per play.

**The energy math.** Whether a loop is truly infinite comes down to net energy.
Count the energy a lap costs (welder ☾ is free; replaying a returned warrior
costs its reduced price) against what the lap refunds (Spirit Reaping 2●, Arc
Disciple 2●, Pyrewatcher's 3● one-shot, Glimpse of Infinity and Pulse of
Sacrifice as burst fuel, Crescendo Channeler scaling with the board). If the
refund exceeds the cost, the loop is unbounded and any drain payoff ends the
game. Conduit of Ashes (0● body, "Abandon this character: Gain 1●") and other
free or near-free warriors are the cheapest fodder to keep the math positive.

## Misc. Role Players

- Pyrewatcher
- Glimpse of Infinity
- Pulse of Sacrifice

Pyrewatcher ("Abandon this character: Gain 3●") is a burst of energy that also
counts as a warrior abandon, so it both refuels the loop and triggers Burning
Revenant. Glimpse of Infinity ("Gain 3●") and Pulse of Sacrifice ("Discard your
hand. Gain 3●") are the explosive fast-energy that lets a loop go off a turn
early; Pulse of Sacrifice in particular dumps your hand into the void, stocking
recursion targets for the welders and Prism Caller while paying for the turn.

- Spent Courier
- Ossuary Overlord
- Sigil Analyst
- Winterbough Monk

These are the value bodies the loops recycle. Spent Courier ("▸Materialized,
▸Dissolved: Draw a card with ephemeral") and Ossuary Overlord ("▸Materialized,
▸Dissolved: Draw a card") draw on both halves of any return loop, making them the
ideal fuel for the welders and Burning Revenant. Sigil Analyst is a fat
▸Materialized payoff ("Draw 2 cards") to land off Prism Caller or a welder, and
Winterbough Monk ("▸Materialized: Return a card from your void to your hand")
adds a non-abandon way to recur a key event like Aftermath Bloom or Shadow March.

- Speaker for the Forgotten
- Shadow March
- Terminus

Speaker for the Forgotten ("5●: Erode 1. That card gains reclaim 0● until end of
turn") and Pit Descender's Erode 3 dovetail with a self-mill plan: erode your own
deck to fill the void with loop fodder, then either replay the eroded cards or
ride Terminus to the empty-deck win. Shadow March ("Materialize all
characters with cost 2● or less in your void which dissolved this turn") is a
mass-return blowout for the cheap-warrior shell — abandon a board to your outlets,
then bring it all back at once, re-firing every ▸Materialized trigger. This is the
overlap with the Wake the Fallen / Shadow March combo, which runs many of the
same abandon outlets and cheap bodies.

## Defensive and Disruptive Pieces

- Vault Infiltrator
- Ordained Collapse
- Eclipse Herald

Because the deck wants to set up over a few turns and then go off, a little
disruption protects the plan. Vault Infiltrator ("Players may only play one card
per turn") slows an opponent to a crawl while you assemble; note it taxes you
too, so deploy it once your engine runs on free materializations rather than
hard-cast cards. Ordained Collapse ("Dissolve each character with cost X●") is a
clean sweeper that can also intentionally dissolve your own cheap warriors to
trigger Burning Revenant and the Strikers en masse. Eclipse Herald ("3●: Store
1⧗" / "X⧗: Dissolve an enemy with ✦ X or less") is repeatable removal that keeps
opposing challengers off your engine while it works.

## Overlap with Warrior Aggro

Warrior Combo and Warrior Aggro are two faces of one warrior shell and share most
of their cards. Both lean on Tethered Hollow, Sundown Ronin, and Dreadmount
Sovereign for cost reduction; both want a wide board for Skyflame Commander,
Momentum's Edge, Shadow Reflection, and the Forge-Twin / Voidsire / Crescendo
Channeler figment makers; and both run Aftermath Bloom, Mirror Protocol, Spent
Courier, Ossuary Overlord, and Colossal Convergence. The split is the finish:
Warrior Aggro positions a wide, pumped board as challengers and scores spark as
⍟ directly, while Warrior Combo abandons that same board into a recursion engine
and wins through Silent Avenger drains, Soulrender erosion, or a deterministic
Terminus. Many real decks sit between the two, playing fair warrior beatdown
in games where the loop never assembles and pivoting to the engine when it does —
the natural fall-back plan that makes the archetype resilient against
disruption.
