# Characters with Cost 2● or Less

This is the value engine of the cheap permanent. It is not a tribal deck and it
is not a ramp deck — it tops the curve out low on purpose and wins by replaying
the same cheap characters far more times than their printed cost suggests. Every
1● and 2● body is treated as a renewable resource: play it, get its value,
abandon it to an outlet, and return it from the void to do it all again. The deck
borders on true ramp through its acceleration package, but the ramp is in service
of putting more cheap characters into play, not of reaching expensive threats.
When this writeup refers to "cheap characters" it means characters with cost 2●
or less, the threshold that nearly every card in the archetype reads off of.

## Acceleration

- Heavenward Penitent
- Nexus Wayfinder
- Bloomweaver

The first and most important part of the deck is a way to put extra cheap
characters into play quickly so you can accelerate the gameplan. The goal is the
same as getting to a turn where you can resolve several cards at once: each free
or discounted body is another ▸Materialized trigger, another abandon target, and
another card the void engine can buy back.

Heavenward Penitent (a 1● 1✦ explorer with "The first character with cost 2● or
less you play each turn costs you 0●") is the premier accelerant. Every turn it
turns your first cheap body free, which over a game is an enormous amount of
tempo and, more importantly, an extra play that feeds everything downstream. Its
own Veil 2● keeps it on the board through targeted removal so the discount keeps
coming. Nexus Wayfinder (a 4● 0✦ explorer, "Characters with cost 2● or less cost
you 0●") is the uncapped version: once it resolves, the entire archetype is free.
A board of free cheap characters is what turns a fair value deck into an engine —
every play after Nexus Wayfinder is pure profit, and the only limit is your hand
and the 9-character board cap.

Bloomweaver (a 1● 1✦ visitor with Veil 2● and "When you play a character with
cost 2● or less, gain 1●") accelerates in a different direction: instead of
discounting the bodies, it refunds energy as you play them, so a turn that plays
three cheap characters comes most of the way back. Stacked with a discount, the
refund pulls ahead — a free body off Heavenward Penitent that still returns 1●
off Bloomweaver leaves you with more energy than you started the play with, which
is how the deck chains a long sequence in one turn.

## Replay and Recursion

- Clockwork Conductor
- Starsea Traveler
- Scrap Reclaimer

The best pairing for the acceleration package is one of the void engines that
lets you play cheap characters straight out of the void. These are the heart of
the deck: paired with the ability to play several cheap bodies per turn, they let
you go arbitrarily wide off any effect that fills your void with cheap
characters.

Clockwork Conductor (a 4● 3✦ tinkerer, "You may play characters with cost 2● or
less from your void") is the uncapped recursion engine — once it is in play, your
void is a second hand of cheap characters, and with Nexus Wayfinder beside it
each of those replays costs nothing. Starsea Traveler (a 3● 3✦ explorer, "Once
per turn, you may play a character with cost 2● or less from your void") is the
fair version of the same effect, buying back one body per turn for grindy value.
Both are at their best when something keeps refilling the void — every dissolved
or abandoned cheap character is another card the engine can replay.

Scrap Reclaimer (a 0● 0✦ tinkerer with Awakened, Veil 2●, and "2●, ☾, Discard a
card: Return up to 2 characters with cost 2● or less from your void to your
hand") is the bulk-recursion piece. It enters awakened so it can pay its ☾ cost
the turn it lands, and Veil 2● protects it; each activation rebuilds your hand
from the void two bodies at a time while also feeding the discard a card the void
deck is happy to spend. Steel Abomination (a 3● 3✦ tinkerer) does the same return
on death: "▸Dissolved: Return up to 2 characters with cost 2● or less from your
void to your hand," and it also reads "When a character with cost 2● or less is
put into your void, this character gains +1✦," so it grows while you grind and
pays out a two-card refund when it finally trades. Wreckage Prowler (a 2● 2✦
visitor, "1●: Return a character with cost 2● or less from your void to your
hand" plus "1●, ☾, Abandon a character: Draw a card") buys bodies back one at a
time and converts the surplus into cards. Pathwalker (a 3● 2✦ survivor, "Abandon
this character: Return an event from your void to your hand" plus "▸Dawn: If this
card is in your void, erode 3") rounds out the recursion on the event side and
quietly fills the void from the discard pile every Dawn, so it can go in the deck
with little intention of being cast from hand.

## Abandon Outlets and the Void Fill

- Conduit of Ashes
- Virtuoso of Harmony
- Ruptured Dynamo

Cheap characters are the card draw of this deck — the more bodies you cycle
through the void, the more the engines pay out — so the deck wants outlets that
move cheap characters from play to the void on demand. These are the effects that
keep the recursion engines fed.

Conduit of Ashes (a 0● 0✦ spirit animal, "Abandon this character: Gain 1●") is
the cleanest free body: it costs nothing, makes energy when you abandon it, and
goes to the void where the engines can buy it back. Virtuoso of Harmony (a 2● 2✦
musician, "Abandon a character with cost 2● or less: Gain 1●") is the repeatable
outlet, converting any cheap body into energy turn after turn — point it at a
free body returned by Clockwork Conductor and the energy washes out while the
void refills. Ruptured Dynamo (a 1● 1✦ synth, "Abandon a character: Return a
character from your void to your hand") is the outlet that closes the smallest
loop on its own: abandon a cheap character, return it from the void to hand,
replay it, and every "when a character with cost 2● or less is put into your
void" and ▸Materialized trigger fires again.

Forsaken Pact (a 1● event, "To play this card, abandon a character with cost 2●
or less" then "Discover a character with cost 2● or less and materialize it")
folds the outlet and a fresh body into one card — pitch a spent cheap character
to dig up a better one and put it directly into play. Pathwalker and Wreckage
Prowler both double as abandon outlets, and Vaultbreaker (an 8● 8✦ ancient,
"Abandon a character, reveal this card from your hand: Reduce the cost of this
card by 1●") turns a glut of cheap bodies into a discount on a top-end finisher,
each abandon shaving 1● off its cost while filling the void.

## Mass Return

- Wake the Fallen
- Shadow March
- Heroic Rescue

These effects accelerate you the way the recursion engines do, but all at once:
rather than buying back one body per turn, they return everything that dissolved
this turn. Set up a turn where you abandon several cheap characters to your
outlets, then a single mass-return effect puts the whole pile back into play and
re-fires every ▸Materialized trigger.

Wake the Fallen (a 3● event, "Materialize all characters in your void which
dissolved this turn") is the broadest version — abandon four cheap characters to
Virtuoso of Harmony and Conduit of Ashes for energy, then bring all four back
materialized for one card. Shadow March (a 3● event, "Materialize all characters
with cost 2● or less in your void which dissolved this turn") is the focused
version that keys exactly on the deck's chosen bodies. Heroic Rescue (a 1● event,
"Materialize up to three chosen characters in your void which dissolved this
turn") is the cheap, selective option for a smaller burst. Because materializing
from the void is not playing — it costs no energy, does not use the stack, and
cannot be prevented — these turns are very hard for the opponent to interact
with, and every body that comes back is another challenger and another abandon
target for the next loop.

## Payoffs and Win Conditions

- Abomination of Memory
- Reclaimer of Lost Paths
- Specter of Silent Snow

The deck's inevitability comes from threats that scale off the cheap-character
churn, so that the grind itself becomes the clock. Abomination of Memory (a 1●
1✦ ancient, "This character has +4✦ if there are three or more characters with
cost 2● or less in your void") is a 5✦ body for a single energy the moment your
void fills, which it does almost immediately. Reclaimer of Lost Paths (a 2● 2✦
visitor, "Cards you reclaim are not banished when they leave play" plus "2●, ☾: A
character in your void gains reclaim until end of turn") both grants reclaim to a
void body on demand and removes the reclaimed-banish rule, so cards you reclaim
return to the void rather than being banished — letting reclaimed cheap bodies
loop through your outlets indefinitely.

Specter of Silent Snow (a 4● 4✦ visitor) is the deck's grinding finisher:
"▸Dawn: Abandon a character" feeds it a body every turn for free, and "When you
abandon a character, draw a card and this character gains +1✦" turns every
abandon — its own and your outlets' — into a card and a permanent point of spark.
Left unanswered it outgrows the board while refilling your hand. Ferryman of the
Falls (a 6● 8✦ explorer, "▸Dawn: Abandon a character. Erode 1" plus "When a
character with cost 2● or less is put into your void, draw a card") is the
top-end card engine: every cheap body that hits the void — abandoned, dissolved,
or eroded — draws you a card, so a single big abandon turn refills your hand
entirely. The Forsaker (a 3● 2✦ renegade, "Abandon a character with cost 2● or
less: Gain 1⍟") is the direct payoff that converts the endless supply of cheap
bodies into points, one abandon at a time, and Fathomless Maw (a 3● 3✦ ancient,
"When you abandon a character, gain 1⍟") does the same passively for every
abandon in the deck. Either one turns a stocked void and a fistful of free bodies
into a lethal clock.

Burst of Obliteration (an X● event, "Choose one: Gain X⍟ / Dissolve an enemy with
X✦") is the old-fashioned finisher: with the deck's energy refunds from
Bloomweaver and free plays from Nexus Wayfinder, you can reach a large X for a
single card and simply gain the points to close.

## Disruption and Removal

- Desolation's Edge
- Duneveil Vanguard
- Kindlehorn

The deck's removal doubles as engine fuel because so much of it is paid with the
cheap bodies and the void you are already cycling. Desolation's Edge (a 2● 2✦
warrior, "☾: Store 1⧗" plus "Abandon this character: Dissolve each character with
cost less than or equal to the stored ⧗") banks counters over a few turns and
then sweeps the board of cheap characters when abandoned — a one-sided wrath if
your own threats sit above the stored ⧗, and an abandon trigger on top. Duneveil
Vanguard (a 3● 1✦ visitor, "Discard a character with cost 2● or less: Dissolve an
enemy with cost 2● or less") trades a cheap card from hand — exactly the resource
the void engines want in the void — for a kill. Kindlehorn (a 3● 1✦ visitor,
"When you abandon a character, store 1⧗" plus "X⧗: Dissolve an enemy with X✦")
banks a counter on every abandon and spends them as scaling removal, so the more
the deck loops the more it can kill.

Ordained Collapse (a 3●,X event, "Dissolve each character with cost X●") is a
tunable sweeper that clears a chosen point on the opponent's curve while leaving
yours intact. Astral Angler (a 3● 3✦ explorer, "2●, Discard a character: Dissolve
an enemy with 2✦ or less" plus "1●: Return an ally to hand") is a removal body
that also bounces your own cheap characters to hand to re-trigger their
▸Materialized abilities. Shatter the Frail (a 1● event, "Dissolve an enemy with
1✦ or less" plus Reclaim 1●) and Whisper of the Past (a 1● event, "An event in
your void gains reclaim until end of turn" plus a self-recurring clause that puts
it back on top of your deck for eroding 3) keep cheap interaction flowing turn
after turn from the void.

## Utility and Card Flow

- Evacuation Enforcer
- Vaultbreaker

The deck stalls without a way to convert its surplus into fresh cards, and a
couple of utility pieces fill that role. Evacuation Enforcer (a 1● 1✦ trooper
with Awakened and "☾: Draw 2 cards, then discard 3 cards") enters awakened so it
digs the turn it lands, and the discard is upside here — it is exactly how you
load the void with cheap bodies for the recursion engines to replay. Vaultbreaker
is the top-end the abandon outlets pay for, arriving as an 8✦ body once enough
cheap characters have shaved its cost.

Many of these cards overlap with neighboring decks. The abandon outlets, mass
returns, and The Forsaker / Ruptured Dynamo engines are shared with the Abandon
deck, which uses the same loop of feeding cheap characters to outlets, returning
them, and abandoning again. The void-recursion package overlaps with Survivors,
which runs Scrap Reclaimer, Pathwalker, Evacuation Enforcer, Whisper of the Past,
and Shatter the Frail for the same grinding, attritional reasons. The strength of
the cheap-character engine is that those payoffs come for free alongside cards a
value deck wanted to play anyway.
