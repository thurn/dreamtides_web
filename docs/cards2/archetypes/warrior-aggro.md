# Warrior Aggro — Deep Dive

Warrior Aggro is the aggressive face of the warrior theme: the deck that wants
to spew its hand into the back rank over the first two turns, push a glut of
cheap warriors into the front rank, and turn that width into ⍟ before the
opponent stabilizes. The plan is straightforward, which is part of its appeal —
play a deck full of warriors that care about warriors, deploy them faster than
anyone expects, and let the type payoffs do the math. It is the largest of the
warrior strategies and the most forgiving to draft, but it rewards a deck that
keeps a tight curve and a real density of payoffs over a pile of bodies that do
nothing on their own.

## Explosive deployment

What makes the deck frightening is the speed at which it empties a hand. Where
other decks spend the early turns building toward something, Warrior Aggro is
trying to commit four or five characters before the opponent has a board at all.

The premier enabler is **Tethered Hollow** — "The first warrior you play each
turn costs 3● less." A 1● body that turns one warrior per turn into a discount,
it is the card that lets a hand of 2● and 3● warriors come down a turn ahead of
schedule. It is at its best when the rest of the curve is built to take
advantage of it: the discount is wasted on a free body but enormous on a
mid-cost payoff, so lead with the cheap warriors and save the discount for the
expensive one.

Behind it sit the energy producers that convert a wide board back into ●.
**Wolfbond Chieftain** is a 1● Awakened body — it enters ready, so it can pay its
☪ cost the turn it lands — with "☪: Gain 1● for each allied warrior." On a board
of three or four warriors it is a one-turn energy explosion that refuels a
second wave. **Crescendo Channeler** does the same job from the four-slot, adding
"2●, ☪: Gain 1● for each allied warrior" on top of a ▸Dawn that materializes a
1✦ warrior figment each turn — it both widens the board and cashes that width in
for energy. The burst-energy texture rounds out with **Lunar Hart**
("▸Materialized, Dawn: Gain 2●.") and **Emberfang** ("▸Dawn: Gain 2●. The
opponent gains 3⍟."). Emberfang is the deal-with-the-devil accelerant: a
free 0● body that fuels a turn-two dump but hands the opponent 3⍟ every Dawn, so
it belongs only in the fastest builds that intend to close before that tax adds
up.

A characteristic opening is Tethered Hollow into a discounted 3● warrior plus a
Wolfbond Chieftain, with the Chieftain's energy financing a fourth body the same
turn. By the time the opponent untaps you already have a front rank and the
payoffs to make it lethal.

## Payoffs: Support anthems and type scaling

Width is only as good as what scales off it. The top of the payoff list is the
Support anthems that turn the back rank into a spark factory for the front.

**Skyflame Commander** is the headline — "Support – Supported characters have
+1✦ for each allied warrior." From the back rank it pumps the up-to-two
front-rank warriors it sits behind by the full warrior count, so on a wide board
a single Commander can swing a lane by several ✦. Because a Support benefit that
lands on a figment stack applies to **each** figment in the stack, the same
Commander behind a stack of warrior figments adds its bonus per figment — the
anthem and the figment makers are built for each other. **Nocturne Strummer**
backs it up with a flat "Support – Supported characters have +2✦," plus
"❖ – 1●: Move this character to an unoccupied character position" so it can slide
behind whichever lane needs the boost during the Day phase.

The type-scaling payoffs reward simply playing more warriors. **Company
Commander** grows itself — "When you play a warrior, this character gains +1✦" —
and the gain is permanent, so a Commander that survives a few turns of
deployment becomes a finisher on its own. **Assault Leader** widens the bonus to
the whole team: "▸Dawn: Each allied warrior gains +1✦," compounding every turn
it stays in play. **Momentum's Edge** is the flexible anthem — "▸Materialized:
Choose a character type. This character is that character type. Characters of the
chosen type have +1✦" — name warrior and it becomes a team-wide +1✦ that also
counts itself. **Ride of the Vanguard** is the burst finisher: "❖ – ☪: Give an
ally +1✦ for each allied warrior until end of turn," a one-shot pump that can
push an unpaired challenger to a lethal ⍟ swing during the Day phase.

For one-shot reach, **Shadow Reflection** is a 1● event reading "Give an ally
+1✦ for each allied warrior" — the Cranial-Plating-style "put it all on one
attacker" play, dropped on a front-rank warrior the turn you want to score big.

## Figment makers keep the board wide

The type payoffs all scale on warrior count, so the deck wants engines that keep
producing bodies. **Worldbreacher** is the cheapest — a 1● body with "☪:
Materialize a 1✦ warrior figment" that turns each turn into another warrior for
the count (note it enters exhausted, so the first figment comes the following
turn). **Crescendo Channeler** adds a figment every ▸Dawn for free. **Inferno's
Herald** is the payoff that converts a grown body into a wave: "▸Dawn: This
character gains +1✦" builds it up, and "▸Dissolved: Materialize a 1✦ warrior
figment for each ✦ this character has" means trading it away in a challenge
refills the board with figments, often more than the opponent removed.

Because figments of the same type share a single position as a stack, these
makers do not eat the 9-character cap the way separate bodies would — a stack of
warrior figments occupies one slot, displays its total spark, and challenges
together. That makes the figment plan a clean fit with the Support anthems
above: one Skyflame Commander behind a fat warrior-figment stack is adding +1✦
per figment per warrior.

## Two finisher creatures the team enables

A couple of warriors are mediocre on their own and excellent once the board is
wide. **Ironclad Holdout** is the Signal-Pest analog — a 1● body with
"▸Challenge: Each challenging ally gains +1✦." Played alone it is a 1✦ irritant;
played alongside three other challengers it adds +1✦ to each of them as the
Night phase opens, turning a coordinated front rank into a much bigger ⍟ swing.
**Flamestride Rider** protects the assault: "Allied warriors cannot be dissolved
while challenging" means the opponent's blockers trade nothing — your warriors
win or stalemate every defended lane and keep coming. **Sundown Ronin** taxes
from the curve angle ("Warriors cost you 1● less"), and the top-end payoff
slot is filled by **Runebound Champion** — a 5● body that "costs 1● less for each
allied warrior," landing for a song on a developed board.

## Card flow and grinding

The deck's real weakness is running out of gas — drawing all enablers and no
payoffs, or simply emptying a hand and topdecking. The pool answers with
warriors that keep cards flowing. **Invoker of Myths** ("Once per turn, when you
materialize a warrior, draw a card") turns each turn's first deployment into a
card, exactly the Welcoming-Vampire/Losheel role of refilling as you go.
**Spent Courier** and **Ossuary Overlord** are the cheap value bodies that
replace themselves on both ends: Spent Courier reads "▸Materialized, Dissolved:
Draw a card with ephemeral" and Ossuary Overlord "▸Materialized, Dissolved: Draw
a card," so deploying them and later trading them away each cantrip. **Company
Commander** doubles as a payoff and a reason every body you play matters.

For a heavier late game, **Key to the Moment** ("Return all but one allied
character to hand. Draw a card for each character returned.") is the
Paradoxical-Outcome refuel — bounce a developed board for a fistful of cards,
then redeploy it with the discount enablers, re-firing every ▸Materialized in the
process. It is a reset that turns a stalled board back into a hand.

## Tutors and recursion for staying power

When the aggression stalls, the deck can pivot toward the warrior engines it
shares with Warrior Combo. **Aftermath Bloom** is a warrior tutor — "To play
this event, abandon a warrior. Discover a warrior with higher cost, then
materialize it" — trading a spent 1✦ body for a bigger threat straight into play.
**Mirror Protocol** does it repeatably: "☪, Abandon a warrior: Discover a warrior
with cost 1● higher and materialize it," a ladder that climbs the curve one
warrior at a time, the Oswald-style chain that both digs and develops.

The recursion line lives in the void-erosion bodies. **Pit Descender**
("▸Materialized: Erode 3" plus "☪, Abandon a warrior: Materialize a warrior with
cost 2● or less from your void") fills the void and then re-buys cheap warriors
out of it, a self-contained value loop that does not need the rest of the combo.
**Dream Garden Visitor** ("▸Materialized, Dawn: Erode 1. Once per turn, you may
play a warrior from your void") and **Echo Technician** ("▸Materialized: Erode 4"
plus "☪: You may play an event from your void") provide the same recur-from-the-
void texture for grinding games. **Forsworn Champion** and **Colossal
Convergence** are the abandon-fueled growth finishers borrowed from the combo
side: Forsworn Champion ("❖❖ – Abandon a warrior: This character gains +1✦")
sacrifices the board to grow itself for a lethal swing, and Colossal Convergence
("❖❖ – ☪, Abandon a character: Give an ally +X✦ where X is the abandoned
character's ✦") dumps a dying board's spark onto one unpaired challenger.

A note on **Vault Infiltrator** ("Players may only play one card per turn"): it
is a symmetric tax that hurts the slower deck more than the aggressor, but it
fights the deck's own flood-the-board plan, so it belongs in a leaner,
prison-leaning build rather than the all-out version.

## Overlap with Warrior Combo

Warrior Aggro and Warrior Combo are the two ends of one theme and share a wide
seam of cards. The figment engines (**Voidsire** — "When you play a warrior,
materialize a 1✦ warrior figment" — **Crescendo Channeler**, **Spellweaver**,
**Forge-Twin**) generate the wide boards both decks want; the aggressive build
turns that width into ⍟, while the combo build feeds it to an abandon outlet
like **Spirit Reaping** ("Abandon a warrior: Gain 2●") or **Infernal Ascendant**
("Abandon another character: Gain 1●") to loop warriors for energy and triggers.
The tutors (**Aftermath Bloom**, **Mirror Protocol**) and the void engines
(**Pit Descender**, **Spent Courier**, **Ossuary Overlord**) appear on both
lists, and the abandon-payoff finishers (**Forsworn Champion**, **Colossal
Convergence**, **Sundown Ronin**, **Shadow Reflection**) span the two. The
practical distinction is the win plan: Warrior Aggro scores spark with a wide
front rank backed by Support anthems and closes before the opponent sets up,
whereas Warrior Combo holds the board to grind near-infinite value through
abandon loops, drains like **Silent Avenger** ("When a character is dissolved,
gain 1⍟"), and erosion like **Soulrender** ("Abandon a character: Chosen player
erodes X, where X is that character's cost"). When the aggressive draw fails to
close, leaning on the shared engines is the natural fallback — which is why most
warrior drafters keep a foot in both camps.
