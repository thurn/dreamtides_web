# Tides4 decklists

The preconstructed decks ("tides") the `?algo=tides4` draft-pool variant
combines into draft pools. A pool is built by combining a few tides: the
DreamAvatar's signature tide is always joined, a random subset of its theme
(facet) tides is drawn, and broad tides top the pool up; the combined bag is
shuffled and the first 150 cards are dealt (never more than 2 copies of a
card). `tides4` is the human-legible counterpart of `sigseed` — each facet tide
is a single-anchor `sigseed` pool, and drawing a random subset of a
DreamAvatar's facets reproduces the variety `sigseed` gets from growing each
pool from a random subset of its signature cards. A signatureless DreamAvatar
draws its subset from the whole facet library, so each run leans toward a
different coherent archetype.

GENERATED FILE — regenerated by `npm run bake-tides4` together with
`data/tides4.jsonc` (the machine-readable artifact, keyed by card UUID).

## Kell Tarn signature — Sacrifice Draw Engine

`tide-sig-01` — signature tide, 84 distinct cards, 108 copies, Kell Tarn's signature

*A survivor-sacrifice engine that converts dissolved allies into card draw, energy, and replacement figment tokens, distinguished from its nearest sibling by prioritizing card advantage and resource accumulation over the aggressive spark-scaling and interrupt removal that sibling tide-sig-02 builds toward.*

Tide-sig-01 is a heavily character-weighted deck (84 characters, 26 events) built around a self-sustaining sacrifice loop: low-cost survivors enter play, generate value on dissolution, and leave behind figment tokens that fuel further sacrifices. The central draw engine pairs Deathwalker ("when an allied survivor is dissolved, draw a card"), Soulreaver ("when a non-figment ally is dissolved, draw a card"), and Scuttled Fortune ("draw a card for each ally that dissolved this turn") with Dustborn Veteran and Defiant Holdout, which materialize survivor figments whenever non-figment allies dissolve — keeping the board replenished while churning through the deck. Energy is generated throughout this loop via Nightmare Manifest ("abandon a character: gain 1●"), Entropy Spike ("dissolved: gain 1● or gain 1 maximum ●"), Volcanic Channeler ("when an ally is dissolved, gain 1●"), and Exiles of the Last Light ("abandon another character: gain 1●"), letting the dissolve-and-replace cycle compound rather than stall. The companion hand-disruption package — Lurking Dread, Dreaming Groves, Tidecaller, and Wasteland Arbitrator — taxes the opponent's options while the sacrifice engine assembles. Compared to the closely related tide-sig-02, which doubles down on spark-scaling payoffs (Cinderheart growing with each abandon, Dreadweaver converting two sacrifices into +2✦ and two cards, Together Against the Tide providing interrupt removal after abandonment), tide-sig-01 instead densifies the pure draw-and-energy reward axis, making it a resilient midrange grind deck rather than an escalating threat engine.

## Caedryn signature — Abandon Payoff

`tide-sig-02` — signature tide, 79 distinct cards, 112 copies, Caedryn's signature

*A sacrifice-driven deck that converts repeated abandonment into ✦ scaling, card draw on dissolution, and interrupt removal — distinct from its nearest sibling by centering on abandonment as an active resource that fuels both challenge threat and board control, rather than hand disruption or void-size payoffs.*

Tide-sig-02 is a heavily character-weighted deck (87 of 110 copies) built around the act of abandoning allies as the primary resource. A dense core of 2x cards establishes two interlocking loops: low-cost survivors such as Harvester of Despair and Cinderheart grow their ✦ each time a character is abandoned, turning a stream of cheap sacrifices into a board of mounting threats, while dissolution payoffs — Soulreaver drawing a card when a non-figment ally dissolves, Deathwalker doing the same for survivors, and Scuttled Fortune cashing in an entire turn of deaths — ensure that spending characters translates directly into card advantage. Volcanic Channeler and Nightmare Manifest convert dissolution and abandonment into energy (●), keeping the engine solvent. Dustborn Veteran and Defiant Holdout supply fresh survivor figments whenever the cycle consumes real characters, and low-cost recursion via Veil of the Wastes and Duskwall Delver restocks the sacrifice pile from the void. The 2x copies of Together Against the Tide — an interrupt that dissolves a ≤3● cost enemy on the same turn you abandon — give the deck reactive board control that activates naturally within the gameplan. Compared to its nearest sibling tide-sig-01, which leans toward disrupting the opponent's hand with cards like Lurking Dread and reaches for mass void recursion with Wake the Fallen, tide-sig-02 is more internally focused: it earns its advantages by treating each sacrifice as both a challenge investment (✦ growth) and a card-draw trigger, and it answers enemy threats through interrupt events unlocked by abandonment rather than stripping opponent resources from hand.

## Kragg signature — Abandon ✦ Growth

`tide-sig-03` — signature tide, 104 distinct cards, 110 copies, Kragg's signature

*A wide-board abandon deck that uses character sacrifice to grow ✦ on key threats and drain ⍟ from the opponent, distinguished from its nearest sibling tides by a spirit animal energy package and board-wide ✦ finishers rather than a tight survivor tribal engine.*

This tide is overwhelmingly character-heavy (94 of 110 copies) and built around the act of abandoning allies as a repeatable resource. The six densest cards each reward when characters leave play: Skull Weaver and Silent Avenger convert any ally's exit into ⍟, Avatar of Cosmic Reckoning drains ⍟ on every dissolve while also producing a figment on entry, and Cinderheart accumulates +1✦ for each abandon then cashes that growth as ⍟ when it is itself dissolved. Harvester of Despair and Exiles of the Last Light close a loop where cheap characters are sacrificed for ✦ and energy, which feeds back into deploying more characters. A secondary spirit animal package — including energy-generating one-drops and lords like Ghostlight Wolves (gain ● per allied spirit animal, +2✦ support) and Spiritbound Alpha (mass +3✦ and vengeful for a turn) — provides both the ● to keep abandoning and the wide board that makes the finisher Spirit Bond ("allied characters have +X✦ where X is allied count") lethal. The nearest sibling tides (tide-sig-02 and tide-sig-01) share the abandonment shell but are oriented around the survivor subtype specifically, doubling up Sorrow Watcher (survivor figment generation), Soulreaver (draw on non-figment dissolves), Tidewreck Navigator (survivor void recursion), and Kindred Sparks (cheap survivor payoff) to build a dense recursive survivor flood; tide-sig-03 instead spreads across spirit animals and broader character types, trades the draw-heavy survivor loops for ⍟ drain and ✦ scaling, and closes games with a board-wide pump rather than iterative survivor recursion.

## Vrakmoth signature — Discard Value Engine

`tide-sig-04` — signature tide, 95 distinct cards, 109 copies, Vrakmoth's signature

*A discard-to-value engine that profits from each card thrown away, distinguishing itself from its void-depth siblings by cashing discards immediately into draws and reclaim rather than hoarding the void for late scaling.*

Tide-sig-04 is built around Vrakmoth's loop of discarding cards to refill, treating every discard as a draw and value event rather than a cost. The engine runs through a dense suite of discard-payoff cards: Key Sifter draws back every discarded card, Pattern Seeker and Torchbearer of the Abyss draw (and Torchbearer ramps 1●) on each discard, Unbroken and Wandering Archivist grow or gain ⍟ as you discard, and Shoreline Penitent, Iconoclast, Gleamharvester and Minstrel of Falling Light loot for further value. A parallel layer of cards is designed to be thrown away and recovered: Part the Veil, Seek Answers, Forsaken Skyline, Pinnacle Ascendant, Oblivion Guide and Fell Swoop all gain reclaim when discarded, and a recursion shell (From the Barrow, Keeper of the Lightpath, Dreadwood Emissary, Somber Flockmaster, Weblight Waif) hands void events reclaim so the discarded spells come back. Bulk looters — Grotto Seer, Rubble Diviner, Urban Cipher, Evacuation Enforcer, Starfall Communion, Sanctum Approach — churn the hand to feed all of this, while discard-cost events (Call of Calamity, Spiral Offering, Desperation, Abandon Constraints) convert the surplus into draws and removal. A light event-copy splash (The Ringleader, Twice-Lit Portal, Echo Architect, Cascade of Reflections) and removable interaction (Fell the Mighty, Molten Duel) round it out, but copying is a minor accent rather than the engine. Its nearest discard siblings, tide-sig-06 and tide-fac-32, share the discard package but point it at void-depth payoffs — banishing or counting the growing void for escalating finishers — whereas this tide spends each discard immediately for draw and reclaim value, keeping the loop churning rather than accumulating.

## Seraveth signature — Void Accelerant

`tide-sig-05` — signature tide, 99 distinct cards, 108 copies, Seraveth's signature

*A survivor-heavy tide that deliberately floods the void through repeated erode triggers and cheap character play, then cashes in that void depth for a near-free high-spark finisher while dissolved-ally payoffs keep the hand full — distinguished from its nearest siblings by centering on void quantity as a resource rather than discard looping or abandon-for-energy ramp.*

Tide-sig-05 is built around an unusually deep void-filling engine and the payoffs that exploit it. The backbone consists of characters that erode on materialization or dissolution — Searcher in the Mists erodes 4 both when it enters and when it is dissolved, Fargazer erodes 1 every time any character is played and then reclaims itself to repeat the effect, and Ashwalker erodes 3 on materialization. That steady void accumulation exists to enable the tide's marquee threat: Paradox Enforcer, which costs just 1● and hits for 7✦ once you banish 7 cards from your void, and can even be played from the void itself. Supporting the board in the meantime, Veil of the Wastes recurs from the void every time you materialize any character costing 2● or less, and Kindred Sparks plays for 1● whenever an allied survivor is in play, letting the tide sustain board presence cheaply while continuing to erode. When allies do fall, Soulreaver draws a card for each non-figment dissolution and Dustborn Veteran replaces dissolved survivors with figment tokens, converting challenge losses into a growing board. Compared to the Kael Voss tide — which shares the survivor and void-recursion shell but pivots into a discard-looping subgame with cards that draw, gain ●, or grow in power each time a card is discarded — Seraveth's tide treats the void as fuel to be burned rather than a trigger to be repeatedly activated; and unlike the Vaela tide, which uses abandonment to ramp energy through Nightmare Manifest and Exiles of the Last Light, this tide's abandon effects are secondary tools (Obliterator of Worlds, Kindlehorn) grafted onto a primary plan of out-eroding the opponent and slamming a seven-spark threat for one energy.

*A warrior-dense energy engine that sacrifices cheap warriors via Blood Reaver to fuel accelerated deployment of expensive warriors, distinguished from its siblings by pairing that abandon-to-energy loop with steep cost-reduction and board-wide +✦ scaling rather than dissolved-triggered recursion or figment token generation.*

Tide-neu-04 is built almost entirely of warriors (28 of 30 cards are characters, nearly all warrior subtype) and operates around a central abandon-for-energy loop: Blood Reaver (the tide's 2x anchor) converts any warrior into 2● on demand, while Sundown Ronin, Tethered Hollow, Revered Prince, and Dreadmount Sovereign stack cost reductions that let the recovered energy place expensive warriors well ahead of schedule. That energy surplus feeds into a board-wide ✦ payoff layer — Inspiring Templar, Assault Leader, Skyflame Commander, and the fast event Shadow Reflection all scale with the number of allied warriors, so the correct pattern is to sacrifice one or two cheap bodies for fuel and then flood the board to maximize the pump effects. The two upgrade events (Aftermath Bloom and Mirror Protocol) both require abandoning a warrior as their cost and return a discovered warrior of higher cost directly into play, extending the sacrifice loop into a mana-efficient curve-climbing engine. The nearest sibling, tide-fac-17, shares Blood Reaver and some of the warrior shell but centers its identity on dissolved-triggered recursion — Cinderblade Legionnaire and Starbound Striker (each at 2x) return warriors from the void on dissolution, enabling a loop-back gameplan — while also investing heavily in Blade of Unity figment tokens via Forge-Twin and Gilded Catalyst. Tide-fac-29 similarly pairs warrior sacrifice with figment production and sacrifice-for-card-draw outlets (Grim Reclaimer, Blood Moon Ronin). Tide-neu-04 does neither: it has no figment-production payoffs and no dissolved recursion at 2x, instead treating every sacrifice purely as an energy conversion that reduces the cost of the next, bigger warrior and widens the board for a single decisive +✦ burst.

## Broad: Guide of Lost Souls / Corrupted Host — Abandon Spark Removal

`tide-neu-05` — neutral tide, 23 distinct cards, 28 copies

*A self-sacrifice engine that stacks ✦ on individual characters through repeated abandonment, then cashes those stacks out as ⍟ income and targeted dissolve removal — distinguishing it from its nearest sibling, which collects ⍟ passively from dissolution events without building toward a ✦-scaling payoff.*

tide-neu-05 is an almost entirely character-based deck (27 of 30 cards) built around the act of abandoning allies to grow the power of specific threats while simultaneously dismantling the enemy board. The central engine runs through two doubled characters: Harvester of Despair (abandon another → this gains +1✦) and Cinderheart (gains +1✦ per abandon, then on its own dissolution converts that ✦ total directly into ⍟ income). Saltless Mariner and Obliterator of Worlds extend the loop — the former yields 1⍟ per abandon activation, while Obliterator banks ⧗ from each abandonment and spends accumulated ⧗ to dissolve any enemy whose ✦ does not exceed the stored count, providing scalable board control. Against its nearest sibling tide-neu-07, which also runs Silent Avenger but centers on Skull Weaver and broad dissolution-triggered ⍟ with no ✦-stacking pieces, tide-neu-05 is distinguished by its active ✦-growth loop: the deck must deliberately sequence abandons onto Harvester and Cinderheart to reach both its removal threshold (Obliterator, Kindlehorn) and its ⍟ burst, making it a more deliberate and higher-ceiling sacrifice engine rather than a passive dissolution collector.

## Broad: Part the Veil / Catastrophe Broker — Broad Discard Engine

`tide-neu-06` — neutral tide, 27 distinct cards, 30 copies

*This tide discards aggressively to fill the void and then converts that void depth into scaling removal, reclaim recursion, and high-spark threats — unlike its siblings, which use discarding to generate energy or cycle events.*

Tide-neu-06 is a character-dense discard engine built around accumulating a large void and spending it as a resource. The two-copy anchors — Part the Veil, which erodes four cards and recovers two into hand, and Grotto Seer, whose Challenge ability discards the entire hand and draws three fresh cards — establish the core pattern of rapid void-filling through repeated hand destruction. Compared to the nearest sibling tide (tide-fac-32), which channels discarding into figment-token production and direct dissolve events, and to tide-fac-27, which uses each discard to draw a card and generate energy through Torchbearer of the Abyss while building toward event-copy payoffs, this tide runs far fewer events and no token sub-theme; instead, it converts raw void count into removal power and enormous singular threats.

## Broad: Soulkindler / Angel of Memory — Void Cycling Recursion

`tide-neu-08` — neutral tide, 26 distinct cards, 30 copies

*A cheap-character recursion engine that deliberately cycles ≤2● characters through the void to unlock void-count bonuses and draw, then recurs them repeatedly — distinct from sibling spirit animal energy decks that flood the board for Dawn triggers, and from sacrifice-for-removal archetypes that treat abandoning as a cost to destroy enemies.*

Tide-neu-08 is a character-dense deck (27 of 30 cards are characters) built almost entirely on cheap ≤2● bodies that rotate through the void as both fuel and payoff. The central loop involves deliberately abandoning or spending cheap characters to fill the void, then drawing value from Ferryman of the Falls ("when a ≤2● cost character is put into your void, draw a card") and powering up Abomination of Memory (which gains +4✦ whenever three or more ≤2● characters sit in the void). Tide-sig-05 uses abandoning as a cost too, but to fuel dissolve removal (Maelstrom Denial, Together Against the Tide) and ⍟ engines, not to stack the void for recursion bonuses. Tide-neu-08's distinctive play pattern is a mid-game grind loop where cheap bodies continually re-enter the void and return to hand, generating incremental card draw, mana, and a steadily growing threat in Abomination of Memory and Carrion Lord's figments.

Tide-neu-12 is a character-heavy deck (26 of 30 cards are characters) built around maintaining a wide warrior board and converting that board presence into +✦ through several interlocking lords: Skyflame Commander grants supported characters +1✦ for each allied warrior, Assault Leader pumps every warrior at dawn, Company Commander scales when any warrior is played, and Shadow Reflection converts warrior count into a burst of spark on a single challenger. Supporting pieces like Epiphany Unfolded (draw 3, fast/interrupt), Reforged Automaton (draw a warrior on materialization), and Wasteland Holdout (figment triggers → ⧗ → card draw) provide the card flow needed to sustain that aggressive removal-backed board plan.
