# Dreamtides — Archetype Overview

A high-level summary of the major archetypes in Dreamtides. Heavy overlap
between archetypes is the norm in this pool, and most decks blend two or more of
the strategies below.

## Aggro and Tempo

### Spirit Animals
A fast, redundant creature deck built on 1● spirit animals and characters that
care about other characters. The plan is to flood the board and close quickly
with payoffs like Spirit Bond, Mystic Runefish, Field Reverant, Spiritbound
Alpha, and Dreamborne Leviathan, often fetched up with tools like Beacon of
Tomorrow. The "mana dorks" enable explosive starts (turn-1 spirit animal into
turn-2 spirit animal plus Spirit of the Greenwood) and the sheer density of
creatures lets the deck grind through interaction. It overlaps heavily with
Celestial Reverie Combo but can win a fair game on speed alone, making it one of
the fastest decks in the format.

### Outsiders
A tempo shell built on the outsider bounce ability — "▸Materialized: Return
another ally to hand. Move this character to that ally's position." — which
re-buys ▸Materialized triggers turn after turn while sliding a fresh challenger
into the front rank. Vanishing Inquisitor, Driftrider, and Thronebound Arbiter
form the core, backed by cheap interaction: prevents (Toll of Passage, Defiant
Parry, Echoing Denial) and hand disruption (Vertiginous Leap, Lurking Dread)
protect the clock, while flexible bodies like Signal Resonant set up the early
returns. Most outsider payoffs draw cards when they score ⍟ (Vanishing
Inquisitor, Thronebound Arbiter), so victory often emerges as a byproduct of
grinding the opponent out rather than from a dedicated finisher.

### Warrior Aggro
A fast warrior deck that leverages explosive deployment (Tethered Hollow makes
the first warrior each turn cost 3● less, Wolfbond Chieftain and Crescendo
Channeler convert a wide board into energy) to flood cheap warriors, then
amplifies them with Support anthems and engines like Nocturne Strummer,
Skyflame Commander, Shadow Reflection, and Sundown Ronin. Figment makers
(Voidsire, Spellweaver, Crescendo Channeler) keep the board wide, so type-payoffs
like Momentum's Edge and Skyflame Commander scale quickly. It is the aggressive
face of the warrior theme, contrasting with the more controlling/combo warrior
builds in Warrior Combo.

### Events
An events-matter shell that pairs a high density of cheap interactive events —
removal, prevents, card draw — with payoff characters like Gateweaver, Somber
Flockmaster, The Waking Titan, and Echo Architect that trigger whenever you play
an event. The drafting heuristic is to take eight or more 1● and 2● events
first, then focus on the payoffs. It splashes naturally into Storm, Discard, and
control directions.

### Survivors
The primary aggressive/value archetype of the void, built around Kindred Sparks
as its premier enabler — with another survivor in play it replays from hand or
void for 1●. Rather than tribal beatdown, it leverages survivors that do double
duty across discard, abandon, and void themes: Sorrow Watcher and Tidewreck
Navigator draw cards and make figments, Dustborn Veteran turns survivor deaths
into more survivors, and Deathwalker and Thundercatcher convert the recursion
into cards. Kindred Sparks plus an abandon outlet (Infernal Ascendant, Volcanic
Channeler) forms a powerful and resilient value engine.

## Midrange and Value

### Discard / "Madness"
A void-fueled strategy that turns discarding cards into an advantage. The deck
leans on reclaim and "madness" payoffs — cards that gain reclaim when discarded,
such as Part the Veil and Shadowcaller — alongside engines like Minstrel of
Falling Light, Key Sifter, Flagbearer of Decay, and Wandering Archivist, all fed
by outlets like Evacuation Enforcer, Pulse of Sacrifice, and Wheel effects
(Wheel of the Heavens, Starfall Communion). Discarding doubles as void setup,
pivoting into threats that recur or scale from the void like Harrowing Officiant,
Soulkindler, and Paradox Enforcer, and into cards funneled there deliberately
(Shadowcaller, The Rising God). Across the board, discard works as both void
setup and a value engine.

### Abandon
A deck that combines abandon outlets with characters that want to die. The fair
build grinds value through cheap fodder and abandon/▸Dissolved triggers (Silent
Avenger, Skull Weaver, Fathomless Maw, Thundercatcher) backed by premium outlets
like Saltless Mariner, Obliterator of Worlds, Soulrender, and Vaultbreaker. A
more explosive "combo abandon" variant pushes toward storm-off turns using
energy engines (Infernal Ascendant, Volcanic Channeler, Melodist of the Finale)
and figment producers to loop fodder for a lethal swing, a Silent Avenger drain,
or Kindlehorn removal. The void and warrior themes support abandon with extra
outlets and recursion.

### Characters with Cost 2● or Less
A void-and-recursion value engine that focuses on replaying cheap characters
rather than ramping into big threats. Acceleration (Bloomweaver, Heavenward
Penitent, Nexus Wayfinder) plus recursion (Clockwork Conductor, Starsea
Traveler, Pathwalker, Scrap Reclaimer) generates exponential cheap-character
drops, while every abandoned body is replayed for value. Payoffs range from
scaling threats (Abomination of Memory, Reclaimer of Lost Paths) to big
abandon-fueled finishers (Ferryman of the Falls, Specter of Silent Snow). It
also supports loops via The Forsaker and Ruptured Dynamo, which turn an endless
supply of cheap characters into points, cards, or energy.

### Blink
A value sub-archetype rather than a standalone deck, using rematerialize effects
like Passage Through Oblivion, Duskmount, and Starlight Guide to repeatedly fire
▸Materialized abilities for card advantage. It folds into creature shells,
leaning on ▸Materialized value engines (Winterbough Monk, Verdant Wayfarer, Dusk
Duelist, Call to the Unknown) accelerated out by energy dorks, with self-bouncing
bodies (Still Dreamer, Forgotten Factory Titan) extending the loop.

## Combo

### Storm
A card-chaining combo built on "for each card you've played this turn" payoffs.
Broadcast Array and Intermezzo Balladeer convert a long chain into ⍟, Call of
the Lost converts it into a wide figment board, Hatching Ground turns it into
erosion and Fatigue, and Echoes of the Journey refills the chain for free.
Enablers come from energy rituals (Arc Gate Opening, Genesis Burst, Glimpse of
Infinity, Nebula's Wake, Canopy of Stars, Flash of Power) and event-copying
(Echo Architect, The Ringleader, Cascade of Reflections, Cosmonaut of Tides).
The pool deliberately supports many storm variants rather than one fixed combo.

### Celestial Reverie Combo
A creature-storm engine where characters provide both the cards and the energy.
Playing Celestial Reverie or Luminous Ascent refills the hand as characters
enter, and self-bouncing characters (Still Dreamer, Forgotten Factory Titan,
Nomad of Endless Paths) combined with free-cast enablers (Nexus Wayfinder,
Heavenward Penitent, Melodist of the Finale) generate loops of energy, cards, and
materializations. It can be played as a fair value list or as a deterministic
combo finishing on Worlds Await, Hatching Ground, or a board payoff, and
overlaps strongly with Spirit Animals.

### Warrior Combo
The controlling, combo-oriented side of the warrior theme. Recursion engines
(Burning Revenant, Prism Caller, Starbound Striker) paired with an abandon
outlet — especially Spirit Reaping — and "welders" (Dreaming Obelisk, Pit
Descender) loop warriors for value or near-infinite results. Aftermath Bloom acts
as a warrior tutor, abandoning a small warrior to dig up a larger piece, and
Forge-Twin, Crescendo Channeler, and Voidsire generate huge boards and energy.
It is the natural home for the pool's heaviest warrior engines.

### Reclaim Combo
A character combo that pairs Reclaim 0● bodies (Starrunner, Torn Circuit Feeder,
Enginespeaker) with effects that strip the reclaimed-banish rule — Reclaimer of
Lost Paths and Titan of Forgotten Echoes both read "Cards you reclaim are not
banished when they leave play." Normally a reclaimed card is banished when it
leaves play; with that static it returns to the void instead, so a Reclaim 0●
character can be abandoned and replayed endlessly. Funneled through an abandon
outlet (Infernal Ascendant) and a payoff like Silent Avenger or Soulrender, it
becomes infinite drain or erosion. The pieces are individually useful, so
disrupting one does not collapse the deck.

### Wake the Fallen / Shadow March Combo
A loop built on mass-return effects (Wake the Fallen, Shadow March, Blazeguard)
that materialize every character that dissolved this turn, re-firing their
▸Materialized and ▸Dissolved triggers for repeated value. Combined with abandon
outlets and engines like The Forsaker, Ruptured Dynamo, and Keeper of Forgotten
Light, it generates effectively endless triggers and energy: abandon a board of
cheap characters for resources, return them all, and abandon again. It overlaps
with the Characters with Cost 2● or Less and Warrior Combo decks.

### Fading Farewell
A dedicated combo around Fading Farewell, which returns allies to your hand when
they dissolve this turn. With zero-cost characters (Conduit of Ashes) or a free
abandon outlet such as Infernal Ascendant, it produces infinite loops of plays,
dissolves, and triggers — abandon for energy, the body returns to hand, replay it
for free, abandon again — typically closed out by a Silent Avenger drain or a
Soulrender erosion.

### Cindermarch / Shadow Soloist Combo
Engines built on repeatedly awakening characters. Cindermarch awakens your whole
board whenever a character enters play, going infinite with zero-energy character
producers and ☪ abilities that net energy (Crescendo Channeler, Wolfbond
Chieftain); Conduit of Resonance does the same by re-triggering ▸Dawn on every
character you play. Shadow Soloist awakens each allied character whenever you play
an event, pairing with cheap and Offering events plus energy producers to fuel
Storm, Abandon, and other loops, alongside Gearwright, Moonlit Dancer, and
Lanternhearted. Both serve as the awaken backbone for several combo decks rather
than as standalone strategies.
