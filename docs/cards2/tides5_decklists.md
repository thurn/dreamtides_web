# Tides5 decklists

The preconstructed decks ("tides") the `?algo=tides5` draft-pool variant
combines into draft pools. `tides5` runs the exact same algorithm as `tides4`
(the human-legible counterpart of `sigseed`), grown from a different corpus: it
learns ONLY from the known-good decklists catalogued in
`docs/known_good_decklists.json`, discarding every other draft seat.

A pool is built by combining a few tides: the Dreamcaller's signature tide is
always joined, a random subset of its theme (facet) tides is drawn, and broad
tides top the pool up; the combined bag is shuffled and the first 150 cards are
dealt (never more than 2 copies of a card). Each facet tide is a single-anchor
`sigseed` pool, and drawing a random subset of a Dreamcaller's facets reproduces
the variety `sigseed` gets from growing each pool from a random subset of its
signature cards. A signatureless Dreamcaller borrows a random signatured
Dreamcaller's whole pool, so each run leans toward a different coherent archetype.

GENERATED FILE — regenerated by `npm run bake-tides5` together with
`data/tides5.jsonc` (the machine-readable artifact, keyed by card UUID).

## Kell Tarn signature — Sacrifice Draw Engine

`tide-sig-01` — signature tide, 90 distinct cards, 110 copies, Kell Tarn's signature

*A survivor-sacrifice engine that converts dissolved allies into card draw, energy, and replacement figment tokens, distinguished from its nearest sibling by prioritizing card advantage and resource accumulation over the aggressive spark-scaling and interrupt removal that sibling tide-sig-02 builds toward.*

Tide-sig-01 is a heavily character-weighted deck (84 characters, 26 events) built around a self-sustaining sacrifice loop: low-cost survivors enter play, generate value on dissolution, and leave behind figment tokens that fuel further sacrifices. The central draw engine pairs Deathwalker ("when an allied survivor is dissolved, draw a card"), Soulreaver ("when a non-figment ally is dissolved, draw a card"), and Scuttled Fortune ("draw a card for each ally that dissolved this turn") with Dustborn Veteran and Defiant Holdout, which materialize survivor figments whenever non-figment allies dissolve — keeping the board replenished while churning through the deck. Energy is generated throughout this loop via Nightmare Manifest ("abandon a character: gain 1●"), Entropy Spike ("dissolved: gain 1● or gain 1 maximum ●"), Volcanic Channeler ("when an ally is dissolved, gain 1●"), and Exiles of the Last Light ("abandon another character: gain 1●"), letting the dissolve-and-replace cycle compound rather than stall. The companion hand-disruption package — Lurking Dread, Dreaming Groves, Tidecaller, and Wasteland Arbitrator — taxes the opponent's options while the sacrifice engine assembles. Compared to the closely related tide-sig-02, which doubles down on spark-scaling payoffs (Cinderheart growing with each abandon, Dreadweaver converting two sacrifices into +2✦ and two cards, Together Against the Tide providing interrupt removal after abandonment), tide-sig-01 instead densifies the pure draw-and-energy reward axis, making it a resilient midrange grind deck rather than an escalating threat engine.

## Caedryn signature — Abandon Payoff

`tide-sig-02` — signature tide, 85 distinct cards, 110 copies, Caedryn's signature

*A sacrifice-driven deck that converts repeated abandonment into ✦ scaling, card draw on dissolution, and interrupt removal — distinct from its nearest sibling by centering on abandonment as an active resource that fuels both combat threat and board control, rather than hand disruption or void-size payoffs.*

Tide-sig-02 is a heavily character-weighted deck (87 of 110 copies) built around the act of abandoning allies as the primary resource. A dense core of 2x cards establishes two interlocking loops: low-cost survivors such as Harvester of Despair and Cinderheart grow their ✦ each time a character is abandoned, turning a stream of cheap sacrifices into a board of mounting threats, while dissolution payoffs — Soulreaver drawing a card when a non-figment ally dissolves, Deathwalker doing the same for survivors, and Scuttled Fortune cashing in an entire turn of deaths — ensure that spending characters translates directly into card advantage. Volcanic Channeler and Nightmare Manifest convert dissolution and abandonment into energy (●), keeping the engine solvent. Dustborn Veteran and Defiant Holdout supply fresh survivor figments whenever the cycle consumes real characters, and low-cost recursion via Veil of the Wastes and Duskwall Delver restocks the sacrifice pile from the void. The 2x copies of Together Against the Tide — an interrupt that dissolves a ≤3● cost enemy on the same turn you abandon — give the deck reactive board control that activates naturally within the gameplan. Compared to its nearest sibling tide-sig-01, which leans toward disrupting the opponent's hand with cards like Lurking Dread and reaches for mass void recursion with Wake the Fallen, tide-sig-02 is more internally focused: it earns its advantages by treating each sacrifice as both a combat investment (✦ growth) and a card-draw trigger, and it answers enemy threats through interrupt events unlocked by abandonment rather than stripping opponent resources from hand.

## Kragg signature — Abandon ✦ Growth

`tide-sig-03` — signature tide, 97 distinct cards, 110 copies, Kragg's signature

*A wide-board abandon deck that uses character sacrifice to grow ✦ on key threats and drain ⍟ from the opponent, distinguished from its nearest sibling tides by a spirit animal energy package and board-wide ✦ finishers rather than a tight survivor tribal engine.*

This tide is overwhelmingly character-heavy (94 of 110 copies) and built around the act of abandoning allies as a repeatable resource. The six densest cards each reward when characters leave play: Skull Weaver and Silent Avenger convert any ally's exit into ⍟, Avatar of Cosmic Reckoning drains ⍟ on every dissolve while also producing a figment on entry, and Cinderheart accumulates +1✦ for each abandon then cashes that growth as ⍟ when it is itself dissolved. Harvester of Despair and Exiles of the Last Light close a loop where cheap characters are sacrificed for ✦ and energy, which feeds back into deploying more characters. A secondary spirit animal package — including energy-generating one-drops and lords like Ghostlight Wolves (gain ● per allied spirit animal, +2✦ support) and Spiritbound Alpha (mass +3✦ and vengeful for a turn) — provides both the ● to keep abandoning and the wide board that makes the finisher Spirit Bond ("allied characters gain unstoppable and +X✦ where X is allied count") lethal. The nearest sibling tides (tide-sig-02 and tide-sig-01) share the abandonment shell but are oriented around the survivor subtype specifically, doubling up Sorrow Watcher (survivor figment generation), Soulreaver (draw on non-figment dissolves), Tidewreck Navigator (survivor void recursion), and Kindred Sparks (cheap survivor payoff) to build a dense recursive survivor flood; tide-sig-03 instead spreads across spirit animals and broader character types, trades the draw-heavy survivor loops for ⍟ drain and ✦ scaling, and closes games with a board-wide pump rather than iterative survivor recursion.

## Vrakmoth signature — Discard Value Engine

`tide-sig-04` — signature tide, 94 distinct cards, 109 copies, Vrakmoth's signature

*A discard-to-value engine that profits from each card thrown away, distinguishing itself from its void-depth siblings by cashing discards immediately into draws and reclaim rather than hoarding the void for late scaling.*

Tide-sig-04 is built around Vrakmoth's loop of discarding cards to refill, treating every discard as a draw and value event rather than a cost. The engine runs through a dense suite of discard-payoff cards: Key Sifter draws back every discarded card, Pattern Seeker and Torchbearer of the Abyss draw (and Torchbearer ramps 1●) on each discard, Unbroken and Wandering Archivist grow or gain ⍟ as you discard, and Shoreline Penitent, Iconoclast, Gleamharvester and Minstrel of Falling Light loot for further value. A parallel layer of cards is designed to be thrown away and recovered: Part the Veil, Seek Answers, Forsaken Skyline, Pinnacle Ascendant, Oblivion Guide and Fell Swoop all gain reclaim when discarded, and a recursion shell (From the Barrow, Keeper of the Lightpath, Dreadwood Emissary, Somber Flockmaster, Weblight Waif) hands void events reclaim so the discarded spells come back. Bulk looters — Grotto Seer, Rubble Diviner, Urban Cipher, Evacuation Enforcer, Starfall Communion, Sanctum Approach — churn the hand to feed all of this, while discard-cost events (Call of Calamity, Spiral Offering, Desperation, Abandon Constraints) convert the surplus into draws and removal. A light event-copy splash (The Ringleader, Twice-Lit Portal, Echo Architect, Cascade of Reflections) and removable interaction (Fell the Mighty, Molten Duel) round it out, but copying is a minor accent rather than the engine. Its nearest discard siblings, tide-sig-06 and tide-fac-32, share the discard package but point it at void-depth payoffs — banishing or counting the growing void for escalating finishers — whereas this tide spends each discard immediately for draw and reclaim value, keeping the loop churning rather than accumulating.

## Seraveth signature — Void Accelerant

`tide-sig-05` — signature tide, 101 distinct cards, 108 copies, Seraveth's signature

*A survivor-heavy tide that deliberately floods the void through repeated erode triggers and cheap character play, then cashes in that void depth for a near-free unstoppable finisher while dissolved-ally payoffs keep the hand full — distinguished from its nearest siblings by centering on void quantity as a resource rather than discard looping or abandon-for-energy ramp.*

Tide-sig-05 is built around an unusually deep void-filling engine and the payoffs that exploit it. The backbone consists of characters that erode on materialization or dissolution — Searcher in the Mists erodes 4 both when it enters and when it is dissolved, Fargazer erodes 1 every time any character is played and then reclaims itself to repeat the effect, and Ashwalker erodes 3 on materialization. That steady void accumulation exists to enable the tide's marquee threat: Paradox Enforcer, which costs just 1● and hits for 7✦ with Unstoppable once you banish 7 cards from your void, and can even be played from the void itself. Supporting the board in the meantime, Veil of the Wastes recurs from the void every time you materialize any character costing 2● or less, and Kindred Sparks plays for 1● whenever an allied survivor is in play, letting the tide sustain board presence cheaply while continuing to erode. When allies do fall, Soulreaver draws a card for each non-figment dissolution and Dustborn Veteran replaces dissolved survivors with figment tokens, converting combat losses into a growing board. Compared to the Kael Voss tide — which shares the survivor and void-recursion shell but pivots into a discard-looping subgame with cards that draw, gain ●, or grow in power each time a card is discarded — Seraveth's tide treats the void as fuel to be burned rather than a trigger to be repeatedly activated; and unlike the Vaela tide, which uses abandonment to ramp energy through Nightmare Manifest and Exiles of the Last Light, this tide's abandon effects are secondary tools (Obliterator of Worlds, Kindlehorn) grafted onto a primary plan of out-eroding the opponent and slamming an unstoppable seven-spark threat for one energy.

Tide-fac-32 is a character-heavy (34 characters, 11 events) discard engine built around a two-stage loop: discard cards to produce 1✦ warrior figments, then abandon pairs of those figments to dissolve enemies up to 4✦. Harborwarden at two copies anchors both halves of this loop, while Hope's Vanguard adds a second discard-to-figment outlet for survivor tokens. The hand-churn side of the engine is driven by Evacuation Enforcer (awakened; taps to draw 2 and discard 3) and Key Sifter (replaces each discard with a draw), keeping cards flowing through the hand at speed. Compared to its nearest sibling (tide-fac-27), which runs Torchbearer at two copies and leans into void-size interrupt removal and event-copy payoffs, this tide is less focused on energy accumulation and removal events and more focused on converting the act of discarding into a board presence that can then be sacrificed to answer the opponent's threats directly.
