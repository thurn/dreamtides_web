# RD-hv-umm2.102-1: "When you return a character you control to hand" triggers on each character you control leaving play for a hand, by any effect or cost, seen as it was just before it left; "to your hand" also requires that you own it
- Ladder: 4 (MTG 603.10a: leaves-the-battlefield abilities look back in time, so an ability sees its own card leave), then 5 for "you return" (the simplest reading consistent with both texts: the returning player is the one whose character it is, as "you discard" and "you erode" are). rules.md § Ability Types → Where triggered abilities work and When a character is dissolved, § Zones → Hand, § Keywords and Effects → Erode, and § Zone Changes supply the normative trigger and replacement rules. The read-only Rust trigger definitions at 0d6afa7dc2eef6048264ffd634da1c2b97886b12 supply no return-to-hand trigger (`rules_engine/src/ability_data/src/trigger_event.rs` has only `LeavesPlay`).
- rules.md: § Ability Types → When you return a character to hand
- Affects: 87aba308-00b4-40d8-a46f-4e6f58a664a8 (Breach Artist), ad76c77a-e456-4e59-bbf7-6bcb56f892ee (Ferryman of the Falls)
- Why: rules.md had no return-to-hand trigger. "You return a character you control" could name the player controlling the returning effect or the player whose character returns. The engine attributes "you discard" and "you erode" to the player whose card moves, whatever caused it, and a return cost is always paid by the character's controller, so the trigger matches each character you control, as it was just before it left play, leaving play for a hand by any effect or cost, the opponent's included. Like "When a character you control is dissolved", the ability sees its own card's return ("another" excludes it). A replacement that banishes a reclaimed character or makes a created one cease to exist means it never went to a hand, so it is not returned; a card returned to a hand from a void never left play. Characters leaving one after another ("return each character you control") are seen while the ability's card is still in play. Ferryman's "to your hand" narrows it to cards you own: a character you control that the opponent owns goes to their hand.
- Mechanics: the `Trigger` member `{ on: "returnToHand", subject, toYourHand? }` (`src/engine/dsl/types.ts`) with builder `whenReturn(selector = characterYouControl(), { toYourHand? })` (`src/engine/dsl/triggers.ts`). The matcher (`src/engine/triggers/matcher.ts`) matches `leftPlay` with `to: "hand"` (the destination after replacements), the subject selector against the character still in play, and with `toYourHand` its owner against the listener's controller; `TRIGGER_EVENTS.returnToHand = ["leftPlay"]`. Breach Artist authors `triggered(whenReturn(), …)`; Ferryman `triggered(whenReturn(characterYouControl({ another: true }), { toYourHand: true }), …)`.
