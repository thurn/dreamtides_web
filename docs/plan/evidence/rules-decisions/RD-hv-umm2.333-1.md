# RD-hv-umm2.333-1: A "when … is dissolved" ability triggers for the player who controlled its card as the character left play, and a floating trigger that existed then triggers even if its duration ended as the character left
- Ladder: 4 (MTG 603.3a: a triggered ability is controlled by the player who controlled its source when it triggered; 603.10a: leaves-the-battlefield abilities look back in time, using the abilities that existed and the objects as they were immediately before the event, so a stolen creature's own "Whenever a creature you control dies" triggers for the player who stole it, and an ability that ends as its source leaves still triggers on that departure)
- rules.md: § Ability Types → When a character is dissolved
- Affects: f95cae7b-6502-4dbe-ae26-16853959d9a3, f68774ce-0e94-489f-90a5-68e03a549ff6, 2e8571c0-4faf-4e7a-a702-26952d2035ee, and the later "when … is dissolved" entities listed in RD-hv-umm2.65-1, under gain control such as 4ef8b2e7-3525-4c25-aa30-f751467f182f; floating "when … is dissolved" triggers such as 8a6fc6ea-9a88-441e-bdc2-d40dfec37481
- Why: rules.md said the ability sees the dissolved character as it was just before it left play, but not who controls the ability when its own card is that character, nor whether a floating trigger whose duration ends as the character leaves play sees it. A dissolved character goes to its owner's void and is controlled by its owner there, so a stolen watcher that matched "a character you control" for the player controlling it would otherwise trigger for its owner, who never controlled the character it saw. The ability, like the character, is taken as it was as the character left play: it triggers for the player who controlled its card then, among that player's abilities, and a floating or delayed trigger that existed then, such as one lasting while its source is in play, triggers on that dissolve, its source's own included. Step 1 is silent (no D-, C-, or P-entry covers control of a departing card's triggers); step 2 offers no rule (the hv-umm2.65 behavior is the defect under review); step 3 does not apply (the Rust engine matches a dissolve against the card in the void, without look-back or a control change).
