# RD-hv-umm2.49-1: Discover looks privately at up to 3 random matching deck cards, puts one into hand without drawing, then shuffles the deck
- Ladder: 2 (prototype behavior: `src/rules/battle/battle-card-effects-table.ts` `sampleDiscoverCharacters` and `resolveDiscoverChoice`, recorded in `docs/plan/evidence/legacy-behavior.md` for Sign of Arrival and Azure Cascade), then 5 for the private look and the draw question
- rules.md: § Keywords and Effects → Discover
- Affects: 910b4cf9-dec7-4e03-af4f-7d5ae342eeba; every card, avatar, and Dreamwell card whose text says "discover", among them 8f5f2e26-44b5-447b-90d0-eaf22ab29fed, f61431f3-33bd-42ff-a229-b4013582e86e, 120ea441-f6ff-434b-973c-bde459f65368, and f0f5449e-01c2-4635-bce1-76b179fc2108
- Why: rules.md said "look at 3 cards from your deck matching a stated criterion, then add one of them to your hand" without saying which 3, what happens with fewer matches, who sees them, or what becomes of the deck. The prototype sampled up to 3 distinct matching cards at random with one seeded draw, offered them as a pick-one prompt, moved the chosen card to hand, and shuffled the rest of the deck; with no match it offered nothing and changed nothing. The Rust engine parses Discover but never resolves it, so it sets no precedent. The engine keeps the prototype's outcome: the offered cards come from the `random:discover` stream among the deck's matching cards, by their current characteristics; the controller chooses exactly one; it goes to the controller's hand; then the deck is shuffled from the `shuffle:<side>` stream, and both sides lose track of every card left in it, since a card shown and left behind would otherwise keep its known position. "Look at" (rules.md) rather than the prototype glossary's "reveal" makes the look private to the chooser (simplest reading consistent with "look at" elsewhere, such as Foresee). The opponent still sees that a card went into the discovering player's hand, as the hand's size shows, but not which card: the `cardDiscovered` event is public and names the card only to a side that can identify it. "Put into your hand" is not a draw, so "when you draw" abilities do not trigger and an empty deck causes no Fatigue. How many cards it offers is the `discoverOptions` battle tunable.
