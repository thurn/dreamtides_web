# RD-hv-7x4l.20-1: A choice with more than one option during automatic resolution ends a mandatory sequence; a forced choice does not
- Ladder: 4 (MTG 104.4b and 732.4: a loop is mandatory only when no player can choose to stop it; one that includes a player's optional choice is that player's to stop), then 5 for what counts as a choice
- rules.md: § Infinite Loops → Mandatory Loops
- Affects: no catalog card yet; any triggered ability with a "you may" effect or another real choice that can retrigger itself
- Why: rules.md defined a mandatory loop as one that repeats while no player can play a card, activate an ability, reposition, or pass, which also covered a self-retriggering "You may trigger this character's ▸Materialized abilities". Declining stops that sequence, so a player who keeps accepting is continuing it by choice, as with an optional loop, and ending the battle in a draw (by an exact repeat or by the 100,000-action limit) would take the choice away. Any answer with more than one legal option, made by either player during automatic resolution, therefore starts the sequence afresh: the actions before it never form an exact repeat with the ones after it, and the count toward the limit starts again. A choice with only one legal option, answered automatically, could not have stopped anything, so it leaves the sequence mandatory. This matches RD-hv-7x4l.9-1, where the same distinction decides whether the opponent had a decision in an optional loop.
