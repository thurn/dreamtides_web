# Powered Synergy Cube — Archetype Overview

A high-level summary of the major archetypes in Caleb Gannon's Powered Synergy
Cube. Archetypes are organized by strategy rather than by color: cross-color
variants (for example "red discard" and "black discard") are treated as a
single archetype. Heavy overlap between archetypes is the norm in this cube,
and most decks blend two or more of the strategies below.

## Aggro and Tempo

### Elves
A fast, redundant creature deck built on one-mana elves and creatures that care
about other creatures. The plan is to flood the board and close quickly with
payoffs like Craterhoof Behemoth, Allosaurus Shepherd, Mirror Entity, and
Elvish Warmaster, often fetched up with Finale of Devastation. Mana dorks enable
explosive starts (turn-1 elf into turn-2 elf plus Gaea's Cradle) and the sheer
density of creatures lets the deck grind through interaction. It overlaps
heavily with Glimpse Combo but can win a fair game on speed alone, making it one
of the fastest decks in the format.

### Ninjas
A blue tempo-control shell that uses ninjutsu to re-buy enter-the-battlefield
creatures (Spellstutter Sprite, Snapcaster Mage, Thieving Skydiver) while
backing them with cheap interaction. Counterspells (Force of Will, Daze, Spell
Snare) and discard (Dread Fugue) protect the clock, and evasive enablers like
Changeling Outcast and Faerie Seer set up early payoffs such as Yuriko. Most
ninja payoffs draw cards when they connect (Ingenious Infiltrator, Moon-Circuit
Hacker), so victory often emerges as a byproduct of grinding the opponent out
rather than from a dedicated finisher.

### Artifact Aggro
A fast artifact deck that leverages explosive mana (Mishra's Workshop, Tolarian
Academy) to deploy a glut of cheap artifact creatures, then amplifies them with
anthems and engines like Cranial Plating, Steel Overseer, Nettlecyst, and
Lodestone Golem. The cube contains around fifteen Constructs, so cards like
Metallic Mimic are surprisingly strong here. It is the aggressive face of the
artifact theme, contrasting with the more controlling/combo artifact builds in
blue.

### Spells
A spells-matter shell (often Grixis) that pairs a high density of cheap
interactive spells — removal, discard, countermagic — with payoff creatures like
Young Pyromancer, Dreadhorde Arcanist, Thing in the Ice, and Krark, the
Thumbless. The drafting heuristic is to take eight or more one-and-two-mana
spells first, then wheel the payoffs. It splashes naturally into storm, discard,
and control directions.

### Zombies
The primary mono-black aggressive/value archetype, built around Gravecrawler as
its premier enabler. Rather than tribal beatdown, it leverages zombies that do
double duty across discard, sacrifice, and graveyard themes: Cryptbreaker draws
and makes tokens, Headless Rider turns deaths into more zombies, and Undead
Augur and Skullclamp convert the recursion into cards. Gravecrawler plus a
sacrifice outlet (Phyrexian Altar, Pitiless Plunderer) forms a powerful and
resilient value engine.

## Midrange and Value

### Discard / Madness
A graveyard-fueled strategy that turns discarding cards into an advantage. Red
leans on madness and recursive payoffs (Rielle the Everwise, Containment
Construct, Squee, Glint-Horn Buccaneer) cast off outlets like Bazaar of Baghdad,
Lion's Eye Diamond, and Wheel effects, often pivoting into aggressive threats
like Vengevine, Anger, and Hogaak. Black instead funnels specific cards into the
yard (Bridge from Below, Vengevine, Scourge of Nel Toth) and exploits flashback
spells that become "free" when discarded. Across both colors, discard doubles as
graveyard setup and as a value engine.

### Sacrifice
A deck that combines sacrifice outlets with creatures that want to die. The fair
build grinds value through cheap fodder and death triggers (Blood Artist,
Yawgmoth, Skullclamp, Mayhem Devil) backed by premium outlets like Goblin
Bombardment, Altar of Dementia, and Greater Gargadon. A more explosive "combo
sacrifice" variant pushes toward storm-off turns using mana engines (Phyrexian
Altar, Pitiless Plunderer, Earthcraft) and token producers to loop fodder for a
lethal swing, a Blood Artist drain, or Goblin Bombardment damage. Red supports
black here with extra outlets and artifact synergies.

### Lands
A graveyard-and-landfall value engine that focuses on putting extra lands into
play rather than ramping into big spells. Acceleration (Fastbond, Exploration)
plus recursion (Crucible of Worlds, Ramunap Excavator, Life from the Loam)
generates exponential land drops, while fetchlands act as cheap card draw and
sacrifice fodder. Payoffs range from inevitability engines (Field of the Dead,
Titania) to direct-damage outlets (Molten Vortex, Valakut, Punishing Fire) and
big sacrifice-fueled threats (The Gitrog Monster, Korvold). It also supports
several infinite-mana combos via Fastbond plus Crucible plus Zuran Orb.

### Blink
A value sub-archetype rather than a standalone deck, using flicker effects like
Ephemerate and Soulherder to repeatedly trigger enter-the-battlefield abilities
for card advantage. It folds into Bant and other creature shells, leaning on
ETB value engines (Eternal Witness and friends) accelerated out by mana dorks.

## Combo

### Storm
A spell-chaining combo present in multiple colors. Blue builds around High Tide,
black plays a vintage-style storm using rituals and recursion (with token storm,
Glimpse loops, and Underworld Breach as alternatives), and red uses
spell-copying (Bonus Round, Krark, Dualcaster Mage) to multiply burn and rituals
into lethal output. Payoffs include Tendrils, Aetherflux Reservoir, Brain
Freeze, and Empty the Warrens. The cube deliberately supports many storm
variants rather than one fixed combo.

### Glimpse Combo
A creature-storm engine where creatures provide both the cards and the mana.
Casting Glimpse of Nature, Rite of Harmony, or Beck refills the hand as
creatures enter, and self-bouncing creatures (Shrieking Drake, Kor Skyfisher,
Whitemane Lion) combined with free-cast enablers (Aluren, Earthcraft, Intruder
Alarm) generate infinite mana, cards, and untaps. It can be played as a fair
value list or as a deterministic combo finishing on Thassa's Oracle, Brain
Freeze, or a creature payoff, and overlaps strongly with Elves.

### Artifact Combo
The controlling, combo-oriented side of the artifact theme. Recursion engines
(Scrap Trawler, Teshar, Myr Retriever) paired with a sacrifice outlet —
especially Krark-Clan Ironworks — and welders (Goblin Welder, Goblin Engineer)
loop artifacts for value or infinite results. Tinker acts as an artifact tutor
into pieces like Thopter Foundry or Krark-Clan Ironworks, and Urza plus Sai
generate huge mana. It is the natural home for the cube's heaviest artifact
engines.

### Persist Combo
A creature combo that pairs persist creatures (such as Kitchen Finks) with
effects that remove -1/-1 counters (Vizier of Remedies, Anafenza, Melira) to
create infinite death-and-return loops. Funneled through a sacrifice outlet and
a payoff like Blood Artist or Goblin Bombardment, it becomes infinite drain or
damage. The cube rewards building this combo from cards that are individually
useful, so disrupting one piece does not collapse the deck.

### Second Sunrise Combo
A loop built on mass-return effects (Second Sunrise, Brought Back, Faith's
Reward) that bring back permanents — especially fetchlands and sacrificed
artifacts — for repeated value. Combined with sacrifice outlets and engines like
Zuran Orb and Valakut Exploration, it generates effectively infinite triggers
and mana. It overlaps with the Lands and Artifact Combo decks.

### Enduring Renewal
A dedicated combo around Enduring Renewal, which returns creatures from the
graveyard to hand. With zero-cost creatures or a free sacrifice outlet such as
Phyrexian Altar, it produces infinite loops of casts, deaths, and triggers,
typically closed out by a Blood Artist or Altar of Dementia effect.

### Untap Combo (Intruder Alarm / Ascendancy)
Engines built on repeatedly untapping permanents. Intruder Alarm untaps your
creatures whenever a creature enters, going infinite with zero-mana creature
producers (Hangarback Walker, Emry) and doubling triggers with Sai and Urza.
Jeskai Ascendancy triggers on noncreature spells and pairs with zero-cost
artifacts (Black Lotus, Lotus Petal) and untap effects (Fatestitcher, mana
dorks, Earthcraft) to fuel storm, discard, and other loops. Both serve as the
untap backbone for several combo decks rather than as standalone strategies.
